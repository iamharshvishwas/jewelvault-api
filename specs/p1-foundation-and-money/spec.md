## 1. Scope, roles and conventions

### 1.1 Scope

- **In scope (milestone M1, billing live):** login with roles (own accounts and server-side sessions in the API); stock (designs and pieces, owner-only weight); customers; bills (read-only; correction by re-issue); payments, silver-fine settlements, refunds and void; derived balances and the ledger view; opening balances and the import hooks; daily rates and the stale-rate warning; photos (stored in the database behind a `PhotoStore` interface); audit log; backups and restore; nightly checks; the API and the cache contract between the screens and the API. **The refund function (X11) is the first thing to leave M1 if the calendar slips** (until then a same-day mistake is a void).
- **Forward-compatible, not built in M1:** edit a bill without losing payments, Order Book, public catalogue, stock search, tags, Excel upload, new document types, partial returns. The schema in Part A leaves room for them (section 17) so they need no breaking migration.
- **Out of scope for this spec:** tax invoices and bills of supply (decision 4), partial returns and credit notes, old-gold purchase, karigar ledger, Hindi interface, privacy-law screens (DPDP, due about 13 May 2027, 18 months after the Rules of 13 November 2025 as QA T7 reads them; a shorter period was proposed but none is notified), e-way bills, the in-app staff screen, the printed layout (task P3-T1).

### 1.2 Words

- **Bill** is what the prototype calls an "estimate" or "invoice" (`Invoice` in `mockData.ts`). It is not a GST tax invoice. `[CA]`
- **Piece** (SKU) is one physical item with a unique code. **Design** groups pieces that look alike.
- **NW** is `customerNetWeight`, the weight used for billing. **Original net weight** is the hidden measured weight (`originalNetWeight`).
- **Fine** is the silver weight owed on a wholesale silver bill, settled later in silver or in cash at that day's silver rate.
- **Piroi** is an amount added per line. As coded it is added as rupees (`BillingPOSClient.tsx:54`). Whether it is rupees or grams is open (O1).
- **Receipt** is money received, silver received, or money handed back (a refund).
- **Advance** is money (or silver) a customer has paid that is not applied to any bill; it is derived, never stored (V2).
- **Re-issue** is cancelling a bill and issuing a corrected one in one step, keeping the money received (X10).
- **Opening balance** is what a customer owed (or had paid in advance) on the day the shop moves to this system (D15).
- **Owner, Biller, Staff, anon** are the four kinds of caller (1.3).
- **API** is the Node.js program on Hostinger that is the only door to the database; the browser never reaches MariaDB. **Service function** is one of the twelve money and stock functions X1 to X12 (section 10): one API call, one database connection, one transaction. **Idempotency-Key** is the request header that carries the repeat-safety id (L5); it is what draft 3 called `p_request_id`.

### 1.4 Conventions (apply to every table and function)

- **S1.** One door. The browser talks only to the API (a Node.js program on Hostinger); the database is reachable only from the API, with credentials that exist only in Hostinger's environment variables (U10, A3). There are no exposed schemas, no generic table routes and no GraphQL (A11). Internal tables that no screen reads directly carry the prefix `sys_` (`sys_counters`, `sys_idempotency`, `sys_import_batches`) so that they cannot be confused with business tables; the other tables that no screen reads directly are `sessions`, `sys_login_attempts`, `sys_job_runs` and `sys_error_log` (D24, D25); `users` (D1) is read only through its allow-list (A4). *Test:* T-D01, T-D16, T-D17. *Source:* PART-A-MARIADB-MAPPING.md section 0; NODE-DESIGN section 0.
- **S2.** Identifiers are native `UUID` (MariaDB 11.8 type, 16 bytes), made by `crypto.randomUUID()` in the API (version 4), never a clock or a count. There is **one** id type everywhere: a foreign key from `UUID` to `CHAR(36)` is refused [S]. A design's id may be generated by the screen before `create_design` runs (X8, P2), so that a retry or a photo upload can name it; every other id and every human number is made by the API (L3). *Test:* T-C01, T-C03. *Source:* hazard 4, BIL-01, INV-09; docs #8.
- **S3.** Money is `DECIMAL(14,2)` (rupees and paise). Weight is `DECIMAL(12,3)` (grams to the milligram). A rate per gram is `DECIMAL(12,2)`. A count is `integer`. No `FLOAT`, `DOUBLE` or `REAL` column or expression exists in any money path. **No money or weight crosses the wire as a JSON number**: requests and responses carry them as decimal strings (`"257612.00"`, `"35.420"`; responses give money with 2 decimals and weight with 3), because a number cannot carry a trailing zero (S4) and loses digits above 2^53; a JSON number in a money or weight field is refused with `JV_INVALID_INPUT`. The API computes with integers (paise, milligrams) and writes DECIMAL text; mysql2 returns `DECIMAL` as text (docs #9). DOUBLE gave the wrong rupee on 9,987 of 20,000 deliberate half-way cases where DECIMAL gave none [S]. *Test:* T-A11, T-A09, T-F02, T-F09. *Source:* hazard 5; docs #8, #9.
- **S4.** Sanity caps are enforced by the API's parsers and again by named `CHECK` constraints, **on inputs and on computed amounts**: a weight is at most 100,000 g; a rate or a piece price is at most 100,000,000; a quantity is 1 to 1,000; a bill has 1 to 200 lines; every line amount (metal amount, labour amount, piroi and other charges), line total, subtotal and grand total, and every payment, refund, discount, additional charge, opening balance and `fine_cash` amount (grams × rate), is at most 1,000,000,000. A value over a cap is answered with `JV_INVALID_INPUT` by the API, never with a database error (a `CHECK` that fires is error 4025 and is treated as a bug, S8). An input with more decimals than its column (more than 2 on a rate or an amount, more than 3 on a weight) is refused with `JV_INVALID_INPUT` too, **by the API alone**: MariaDB rounds `1.239` to `1.24` without any error and a `CHECK` sees only the rounded value [S], so it would otherwise be priced unrounded and stored rounded; the screens round before sending. A trailing zero counts, as the value was sent (`scale()` of the number): `1.230` is refused as a rate and accepted as a weight, and the screens never send one. Negative values are rejected everywhere (except `round_off`, D11). The caps are written as literals in the `CHECK` constraints and also in the API's constants file (`limits`, the only place the code reads them from); test T-F02 reads every `CHECK` from `information_schema.CHECK_CONSTRAINTS` and compares it with the constants file, so the two cannot drift. A `CHECK`, a default, a generated column and a view use built-in functions only, and a `CHECK` cannot contain a subquery or `CURDATE()` (errors 1970 and 1901 [S]), so a rule such as "a date is not in the future" lives in the API; a `CHECK` that evaluates to NULL passes [S], so every column a `CHECK` uses is `NOT NULL`. Every connection sets a strict `sql_mode` (S11), because Hostinger's is not strict and a non-strict session clamps an over-range amount to the column maximum without any error [H][S]. *Test:* T-A09, T-A16, T-A18, T-F02, T-F08. *Source:* BIL-04, INV-03, DAT-10, F19, RF-4, RM-11, RM-12; docs #4, #6.
- **S5.** The business time zone is `Asia/Kolkata`. A bill or receipt carries a calendar `date` (the business day) and a timestamp (when it was saved). Timestamps are `DATETIME(6)` in UTC (every connection sets `time_zone = '+00:00'`, S11). "Today" is computed in the API, in the `Asia/Kolkata` zone, from the API's one clock (never from the server's own time zone, which Hostinger reports as UTC [H] but the code does not rely on), and every function takes the time from that clock, so a test can fix it by injecting a fake clock; the seam is unreachable from any route (the production build offers no way to set it). The clock never returns the same instant twice: a second reading in the same microsecond gets one microsecond more, so two rows made in one transaction never tie and the ledger order of V3 and the stale check of A5 keep working (draft 3 had `clock_timestamp()`, which also advances between statements). Reports group by the `date` columns, never by a UTC timestamp. *Test:* T-B16, T-F07, T-F09. *Source:* LED-08, DAT-11, RM-15; docs #8.
- **S6.** Every table has `created_at`, `updated_at` and, where a person acts, `created_by`, except the three append-only logs `audit_log`, `check_runs` and `backup_runs`, which have the one time column that D16 and D17 give them (`at`, `ran_at`), and the internal tables of D24 and D25, which have the time columns named there. `created_at` and `updated_at` are `DATETIME(6)` columns with `DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP(6)` and `ON UPDATE CURRENT_TIMESTAMP(6)`; the API sets **both explicitly** from its clock (S5) on every insert and update it makes, so a test that fakes the clock fakes every stamp of a row, not only its dates, and the column defaults are a backstop: a change typed by hand in phpMyAdmin also moves `updated_at`, which the polling needs (C4). `NOW(6)` is fixed per statement and `SYSDATE(6)` is the real clock [S]. The columns that name a person (`created_by`, `updated_by`, `set_by`, `confirmed_by`, `voided_by`, `cancelled_by`) are **always set by the API from the session user**; a value sent by a client is refused as an unknown field (A5) and never stored. The audit step ignores an update that changes only `updated_at` (D23). *Test:* T-F02, T-D22. *Source:* F7; docs #8.
- **S7.** Nobody deletes. The API never issues `DELETE`, `TRUNCATE` or `DROP` (the one statement wrapper refuses them and a test lists every hit in the code), **and** the one database user the API uses has the **Delete** and **Drop** boxes unticked in hPanel (*Change Permissions*), so MariaDB itself refuses `DELETE`, `DROP TABLE` and `TRUNCATE` (which needs Drop, docs #3) for everyone who uses that user, Harsh's phpMyAdmin included [H][S]. Rows are archived, voided or cancelled, never removed; the few things that used to be deleted are changed instead: an old idempotency row has its result emptied (L6), a session is marked revoked (U4), and a photo the owner removes keeps its row but loses its bytes (P3). Schema changes keep Alter, Create, Index, References and Trigger ticked. Only Harsh, in an emergency, can tick Delete for the time it takes. (`NODE-DESIGN.md` section 8 item 2, as reviewed, keeps Delete ticked, blocks deletes on business tables with triggers and falls back to a second database for the housekeeping tables; draft 4 does not, because triggers are unproved on the plan, a second database cannot share a transaction with the money tables, and the three housekeeping tables can be kept tidy by `UPDATE`; 18.10.1 and O51.) *Test:* T-D07, T-C02. *Source:* LED-03, DAT-01, INV-09; docs #3, #12.
- **S8.** Errors from the service functions are raised as `JV_*` codes and answered as a JSON body `{"error":{"code","message","details"},"request_id"}` with an HTTP status: `JV_INVALID_INPUT` 422; `JV_FORBIDDEN` 403; `JV_NOT_FOUND` 404; `JV_PIECE_UNAVAILABLE`, `JV_CUSTOMER_ARCHIVED`, `JV_OVERPAYMENT`, `JV_CASH_LIMIT`, `JV_FINE_EXCEEDS`, `JV_BILL_CANCELLED`, `JV_ALREADY_VOID`, `JV_NOT_ALLOWED` 409; `JV_BUSY` 503 with `Retry-After: 1`. Codes new in draft 4, because the API layer now exists: `JV_STALE` 409 (an edit made on an old copy; the body carries the current row, A5); `JV_UNAUTHENTICATED`, `JV_SESSION_EXPIRED`, `JV_BAD_LOGIN` 401; `JV_PASSWORD_CHANGE_REQUIRED` 403; `JV_CSRF` 403; `JV_RATE_LIMITED` 429 with `Retry-After`; `JV_PAYLOAD_TOO_LARGE` 413 and `JV_UNSUPPORTED_MEDIA_TYPE` 415 (photos); `JV_UNAVAILABLE` 503 (the start-up check failed, S11); `JV_INTERNAL` 500 (anything unexpected; the detail goes to the log with the request id, never to the client). The screens map each code to a plain message. What each database error becomes: a lock wait timeout (1205), a statement timeout (1969), a deadlock that still happens after the API's own retries (1213, S12), a full connection pool or a lost connection all become `JV_BUSY`, and **nothing was changed**; a duplicate key (1062) is expected only on the idempotency claim and when numbering skips an imported number (L3), and a duplicate business key such as a `design_no` is `JV_INVALID_INPUT`; a failed `CHECK` or an out-of-range value (4025, 1264) means the API's own validation missed something: answered `JV_INVALID_INPUT` and logged as a bug; a foreign-key error (1451, 1452) and anything else is `JV_INTERNAL`. For `JV_BUSY` the screen retries **once automatically with the same Idempotency-Key**, which is safe because of L5. *Test:* T-C04, T-C15, T-C19, T-E04 and each function test. *Source:* docs #1, #2; SPIKE (a), (e); NODE-DESIGN 4.5, 5.6.
- **S9.** Migrations are forward-only SQL files, one concern per file, run by a small runner that records each file in a `schema_migrations` table. **MariaDB DDL is not transactional**: every `CREATE`, `ALTER` and `DROP` commits at once and a rolled-back `CREATE TABLE` stays (docs #3, [S]), so each file must be **re-runnable on an empty database and on a half-applied one**: `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS`, `ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS`, `CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS` (all tested [S]); a file that fails half-way is run again, not repaired by hand. There is no row-level-security clause to add: a new table needs only its primary key, its foreign keys (`RESTRICT`) and its `CHECK`s, which the catalogue test requires (A7). How a migration reaches Hostinger's database is open (O49). *Test:* T-F04. *Source:* docs #3.
- **S10.** The role of the caller is read in one place only, the route wrapper of A1 and A6 (it looks the user up in `users`, A2); no handler contains a role string. *Test:* T-D10. *Source:* F20, NODE-DESIGN 3.1.
- **S11.** *(new in draft 4)* **Connection rules.** Every connection checkout starts with `SET SESSION` for: `transaction_isolation = 'READ-COMMITTED'`; a strict `sql_mode` (at least `STRICT_ALL_TABLES` and `ERROR_FOR_DIVISION_BY_ZERO`); `time_zone = '+00:00'`; `innodb_lock_wait_timeout = 5`; `max_statement_time = 10` (the nightly and backup jobs set a larger value for their own sessions); and the character set `utf8mb4`. The API never relies on Hostinger's globals, which are: not strict, READ-COMMITTED, lock wait 50 s, statements cut at 120 s, idle connections closed after 20 s [H]. Only session settings are possible, because the user is not SUPER [S]. The pool keeps at most 10 connections (Hostinger allows 75 per user [H]), hands idle connections back after 10 seconds, before the server's 20-second cut, and keeps at most 2 idle; it never trusts an idle one: if the checkout statement finds the connection dead it is destroyed and another is checked out (up to twice; nothing had run yet), a connection the server closed is otherwise replaced without an error, and a connection killed inside a transaction fails its next statement and its `ROLLBACK`, and nothing is committed [S]. Sessions set `wait_timeout` to 120 for themselves (a normal user may). A **start-up check** runs before the API accepts a request: the server is MariaDB 11.x; the isolation level is READ-COMMITTED; the session settings above can be set; the character set is `utf8mb4`; every table the code needs exists; and the user's grants are read with `SHOW GRANTS` and reported (a Delete or Drop right that is on does not stop the API but turns the owner's dashboard line amber, I12). The check is quick, because it runs on every start after an idle stop (H7). If any other check fails the API still starts, logs why, and answers `503 JV_UNAVAILABLE` to everything except `/healthz`. *Test:* T-F08, T-C18. *Source:* docs #1, #2, #4, #5; HOSTINGER-FACTS 2; SPIKE section 3 rule 1; NODE-DESIGN 5.2.
- **S12.** *(new in draft 4)* **One request, one connection, one transaction** (the wrapper `withTx` that every writing call uses). (1) Any error means `ROLLBACK` of the whole transaction, whatever the error: a lock wait timeout (1205) rolls back only the failing statement, so earlier statements would still commit if the code carried on (docs #1, [S]); domain code therefore never catches a database error and continues (the one exception, the duplicate-key probe of the idempotency claim, lives in one helper). (2) A deadlock (1213) re-runs the **whole** function up to 2 more times after 20 to 80 ms of jitter and then answers `JV_BUSY`; the idempotency row, the number and every other change were rolled back together with it, so the retry is safe. (3) The whole call has a **deadline of 15 seconds**, checked before every statement, because `max_statement_time` limits one statement and does not add up across statements [S]; with the statement limit of 10 seconds in S11, no row is stamped more than 25 seconds before its commit, inside the 30-second overlap of C4. (4) Statement parameters are text, numbers, booleans, null or Buffers only: ordinary statements are prepared statements (`execute()`, one `?` for each value), an object parameter is refused, because mysql2's `query()` expands an object into `col = val` pairs [S]; JSON columns are always sent as `JSON.stringify` text and money inside JSON is a string; `REPLACE INTO` is never used (it deletes and inserts silently). (5) No network call (storage, mail, another API) happens inside a writing transaction, because it would hold row locks; the one exception is the backup job's read-only snapshot, which takes no row locks (B2). (6) Money is integers in the API and `DECIMAL` text in SQL (S3). *Test:* T-C19, T-C15. *Source:* docs #1, #5; SPIKE (a), (e), section 3; NODE-DESIGN 5.1, 5.3, 5.7.

# PART A: FOUNDATION

## 2. Data model

All tables are in the one MariaDB database (InnoDB, character set `utf8mb4` and the collation `utf8mb4_unicode_ci` **named in every `CREATE TABLE`**, because a fresh 11.8.9 server defaults to `utf8mb4_uca1400_ai_ci` [W] while Hostinger's default is `utf8mb4_unicode_ci` [H]; with it a `UNIQUE` on text ignores case and trailing spaces [S]; table names are case-sensitive on Hostinger [H], so the code uses them exactly as written). "FK" means a foreign key with `ON DELETE RESTRICT` (nothing is ever deleted, S7). Column names are given in the database's `snake_case`; the screens keep their `camelCase` fields through the cache layer (C1). Type names are MariaDB's: draft 3's `numeric` is `DECIMAL`, `uuid` is `UUID`, `jsonb` is `JSON` (an alias of `LONGTEXT`; a `JSON_VALID` check is added), `timestamptz` is `DATETIME(6)` in UTC, `text[]` is a `JSON` array with `CHECK (JSON_TYPE(col) = 'ARRAY')`, and `boolean` is `TINYINT(1)` (docs #8). Names that are reserved words in MariaDB are not used unquoted [S]: `settings.key` is `setting_key`, and no table is called `lines`, `rows` or `order`.

### 2.1 Reference and settings

- **D1. `users`** (draft 3's `profiles`; the API's own accounts, with no outside login service): `id UUID` primary key; `email` (unique ignoring case; the login name); `full_name` (not null); `role` (`owner`, `biller`, `staff`; a `CHECK`); `active boolean not null default false`; `password_hash` (a self-describing Argon2id or scrypt string, U1); `must_change_password boolean not null default false` (the admin door sets it, U3); `last_login_at`; `created_at`, `updated_at`. Opening rows are **not sales** and **not money received today**: they are excluded from sales figures, from cash totals (V4) and from the cash limit (Z1). *Test:* T-B05, T-B21. *Source:* design decision (O8), F4.

FOR UPDATE` and keyed read in the code is backed by an index**: an `UPDATE ... ## 3. Access rules (the API is the wall)

- **A1.** Every route declares who may call it. Every endpoint is registered through one wrapper (`route()`), which takes an `action` that exists in the permission matrix of A4, or sits on a short explicit list of public routes (`/healthz`, `/readyz`, `POST /v1/auth/login`, and the photo read of P3) or of token routes (`/admin/v1/*`, `/internal/jobs/*`, each with its own token check). A route registered without an action fails the build, and a test lists every registered route with its roles. There is **no row-level security**: MariaDB has one database user for every role (A3), so the database cannot tell a biller from an owner, and **the wall is the API's code and its tests**. (Harsh's phpMyAdmin was always a second door, for the database owner, in the old design too.) The matrix lives in one file and a handler never checks a role itself (S10). *Test:* T-D01. *Source:* PART-A-MARIADB-MAPPING.md A1; NODE-DESIGN 3.1, 3.4.
- **A2.** Roles come from `users` through the lookup of D2, on every request. No code reads a role from a cookie, a token, a header or a request body; the cookie carries only an opaque session ticket (U4), and a body or header that names a role is ignored or refused. *Test:* T-D06, T-D15. *Source:* SEC-02.
- **A3.** **One database user, rights narrowed in hPanel.** Hostinger creates one database user per database (the create form asks for the name, the user and the password together, and a second user for the same database is not offered [H]), so the API, the migration runner and Harsh's phpMyAdmin all use it. In hPanel (*Change Permissions*, 18 boxes) **Delete and Drop are unticked** (S7); Select, Insert, Update, Alter, Create, Index, References and Trigger stay ticked so that migrations can run. The user has no SUPER and no FILE and cannot grant, revoke or create users [S]. Table and column grants work in MariaDB [S] but would not separate the roles here, because every role goes through the same user, so the roles are separated by the API (A1, A4). The user's password exists only in Hostinger's environment variables (U10); `.env` is never committed. *Test:* T-D02, T-D07, T-D29. *Source:* HOSTINGER-FACTS 1, 2.4; PART-A-MARIADB-MAPPING.md A3.
- **A4.** The permission matrix. It is one file with one row per thing a caller can do: each **read resource** (served by `GET /v1/sync/<resource>` and the named read calls of C3), each **direct edit** (`PATCH`/`PUT`/`POST` of ordinary fields, A5), each **service function** X1 to X12 with its route (section 10), and the photo calls (P3). The table below gives the read and edit rows (S = read, I = insert, U = update; "content columns" are listed in A5); a caller who is not signed in, or not `active`, gets nothing in any row. Each resource also has a **field allow-list per role** (`READ_COLUMNS`, `WRITE_COLUMNS`) in the same file: a read names its columns and never uses `SELECT *`, so a biller's customer row has mobile, GST and PAN as in A9 but no response to a biller or staff ever contains `original_net_weight`. **All read roles may read archived rows** (the screens hide them), because the screens and the polling need them to see an archiving. Draft 3 gave this table to Postgres policies; the same expectations are now HTTP tests, one login per role.

Nobody has `DELETE` (S7). *Test:* T-D02 to T-D05, T-D07, T-D08, T-D18, T-D19. *Source:* hazards 1, 2, 4, SEC-02, INV-10, F6, F7.
- **A5.** Content columns can be edited directly: for `designs`: `name`, `description`, `notes`, `tags`, `primary_photo_id`, `photo_ids`, `category_id`, `subcategory_id`, `archived_at`; for `skus`: `gross_weight`, `customer_net_weight`, `stones`, `stone_weight`, `kundan`, `kundan_weight`, `piroi`, `labour_retail`, `labour_semi`, `labour_wholesale`, `location`, `notes`; for `customers`: `name`, `mobile`, `customer_type`, `email`, `address`, `gst_number`, `pan`, `notes`. A per-route input schema lists the editable fields per role (`WRITE_COLUMNS`): a field outside the list is refused with `JV_FORBIDDEN`, an unknown field with `JV_INVALID_INPUT`, and nothing is silently ignored (`skus.status` and `skus.archived_at` change only through X9). The API also rejects: staff changing a design's `material`, `purity` or `selling_method`; a biller changing a customer's `archived_at`; archiving a customer whose balance, fine or advance is not zero; archiving a design that has non-archived pieces; archiving takes the same lock as a money function (the customer lock of X0 item 3 for a customer), so a payment cannot arrive between the zero-balance check and the archive. These are rules in code; draft 3 held them in triggers, and a trigger version is possible if triggers work (O48). A stock edit sends `expected_updated_at` (the value as the API returned it, as exact text) and **only the changed fields**; the API runs `UPDATE ... WHERE id = ? AND updated_at = ?`, and if no row changed it answers `409 JV_STALE` with the current row in `details.current`, so a stale edit changes nothing and the screen reloads; the screen keeps the value **as the exact text** and never turns it into a date (C4). No `Idempotency-Key` is needed for an edit: the same edit sent twice is refused the second time by the stale check. *Test:* T-C07, T-D04, T-D19, T-F05, T-F09. *Source:* hazard 2, INV-01, `EditStockClient.tsx:62-65`, F3, F7; NODE-DESIGN 4.4.
- **A6.** Service functions. Every writing call is a service function X1 to X12 or an ordinary edit (A5), reached only through `route()`: session, then the must-change-password gate (U11), then the matrix row, then the body validation (so a forbidden caller learns nothing about the body), then the handler. Each service function checks the role again as its first step and raises `JV_FORBIDDEN` otherwise (defence in depth). They are Node functions, not stored procedures: creating routines or triggers may need SUPER on Hostinger [U], they are harder to test, and the lock order is easier to keep in one module (S12, X0). `quote_bill` (X7) is also a service function; it reads prices and writes nothing. *Test:* T-D09, T-D10. *Source:* PART-A-MARIADB-MAPPING.md A6; NODE-DESIGN 1.3, 3.1.
- **A7.** Static checks replace the Supabase advisor (MariaDB has no equivalent product). The build fails if: a route has no declared roles (A1); a table lacks a primary key, InnoDB or the collation `utf8mb4_unicode_ci`; a money column is not `DECIMAL` or any `FLOAT`/`DOUBLE` column exists; a foreign key is not `RESTRICT`; a named `CHECK` of D8, D13 or S4 is missing; the list of `UNIQUE` keys differs from the expected list (D19); a locking or writing statement does not use an index (the `EXPLAIN` test); the code contains a statement that starts with `DELETE`, `TRUNCATE`, `DROP` or `ALTER` outside the migration runner (S7); a handler contains a role string (S10); a runtime dependency has an install script or a High or Critical `npm audit` finding. Hostinger also scans npm packages after each deployment and may open a fix pull request that Harsh must review [N]. *Test:* T-D11. *Source:* PART-A-MARIADB-MAPPING.md A7; NODE-DESIGN 8 items 3, 12.
- **A8.** The owner can change another user's `role` and `active` (the in-app staff screen is later; until then Harsh does it through the admin door, U3). **No user can change their own `role` or `active`**, owner included, so the owner cannot lock herself out or promote herself; the route refuses it. Biller and staff have no route that updates `users` at all. *Test:* T-D06, T-D20.
- **A9.** Reading is wide, writing is narrow: a role that may read a resource may read every row; the narrowing is by field allow-lists and functions, not by per-row ownership. This means the **biller reads every customer's mobile, GST and PAN and the whole ledger** and may edit customer details; this is as in the prototype and is listed for Siddhi in O25. *Test:* T-D04, T-D18. *Source:* matches the prototype; F12.
- **A10.** A user who is `active = false` or has no `users` row reads nothing and writes nothing: the lookup of D2 answers `401` on every call, **a session that is already open included**, so a user deactivated in the middle of a shift is cut off at the next call (this replaces draft 3's poll check, C18). *Test:* T-D06, T-D26, T-E01.
- **A11.** There is no GraphQL and no generic table route. The only data routes are the named ones of A4; a path that is not in the matrix, such as `/graphql` or `/v1/sync/sessions`, answers `404`, and no route accepts a table or column name from the client (the names come from the allow-lists). *Test:* T-D17. *Source:* F6; NODE-DESIGN 3.4, 8 item 6.
- **A12.** *(new in draft 4)* **Cross-origin and cross-site requests.** The website and the API are two subdomains of one registrable domain that Harsh controls (O38: a domain used only for JewelVault, with one name for the website and one for the API), so the calls are cross-origin but the session cookie is first-party (same-site). CORS answers only for an exact list of origins (the environment variable `ALLOWED_ORIGINS`; in production only the website's origin; never `*`, no patterns, no preview URLs), with credentials, the request headers `Content-Type`, `Idempotency-Key` and `X-JV-Client`, and the exposed response headers `Retry-After`, `X-Request-Id` and `Idempotent-Replay` (the screen cannot read them otherwise). Three independent CSRF layers: the cookie is `SameSite=Strict` (U12); every call that changes state (POST, PATCH, PUT, DELETE) must carry `X-JV-Client: web`, which makes the browser send a preflight that only our origin passes; and for such a call the `Origin` header must equal an allowed origin exactly (a missing or `null` Origin is refused). Any failure is `403 JV_CSRF`. No state change is reachable by GET. If the cookie test of the Hostinger proof fails (O48, test 4) the name pair is re-planned, or the API is put behind the website's own origin by a proxy rewrite, which Vercel offers (docs #11 area, `NODE-DESIGN.md` 2.2; Cloudflare Pages cannot proxy to an outside API [U]). *Test:* T-D23. *Source:* NODE-DESIGN 2.2, 2.6; docs #14 [N].
- **A13.** *(new in draft 4)* **Throttles.** Login: 5 a minute per client address and e-mail, only when the client address can be trusted, and a **progressive delay instead of a lock**: after 5 failed attempts for one e-mail within 15 minutes (counted from `sys_login_attempts`, which survives a restart), each further attempt for that e-mail waits 1, 2, 4 ... up to 30 seconds before the password is checked, and a correct password still works after the wait, so nobody can lock the owner out. Change password: 5 per 15 minutes per user. Admin door: 10 a minute per address, and 15 minutes' pause after two wrong tokens in a row. Job tick: 6 a minute per token. Photo upload: 30 a minute per user. Photo read: 600 a minute per address (the writer's default; a photo read is the one call with no session). Everything else: 300 a minute per user (normal use is about 8 poll calls a minute [U]). Over the limit: `429 JV_RATE_LIMITED` with `Retry-After`. Which header carries the visitor's address behind Hostinger's proxy is unknown [U] (O48, test 5); if it cannot be trusted the per-address rows are dropped and only the per-e-mail delay remains. *Test:* T-D24. *Source:* NODE-DESIGN 2.5; the photo-read figure is draft 4's own.
- **A14.** *(new in draft 4)* **Responses leak nothing.** `JV_INTERNAL` carries no SQL, stack or table name; every data response carries `Cache-Control: no-store` (the photo files excepted, P3) and `X-Request-Id`; the standard security headers are set (helmet); the logs redact cookies, authorization and password fields and never hold a mobile, PAN or GST number; the owner-only weight never appears in a response to a biller or staff (D9). A request body is limited to 1 MiB (a photo upload to 2 MiB and 16 KiB), unknown JSON keys are refused, receiving a request may take 30 seconds, and an ordinary call that takes longer than 20 seconds is answered `503 JV_BUSY` (130 seconds for the job tick) while the transaction deadline of S12 stops the work. *Test:* T-D28, T-D03. *Source:* NODE-DESIGN 4.0, 8 items 7, 11.

## 4. Login, sessions, photos, audit and numbering

### 4.1 Login and sessions

- **U1.** Login is e-mail and password through the API's own route `POST /v1/auth/login`; there is no outside login service. The password is stored as a self-describing **Argon2id** hash made with Node's built-in `crypto.argon2` (docs #10), starting at 64 MiB of memory, 3 passes and parallelism 1 and tuned on Hostinger to roughly 150 to 400 ms a hash; if Node 24 or `crypto.argon2` is not available on the plan (O48) the fallback is **scrypt** (N = 2^17, r = 8, p = 1, with `maxmem` raised above its 32 MiB default, which 128 MiB needs). At most 2 hashes run at the same time. A wrong e-mail and a wrong password give the same answer, `JV_BAD_LOGIN` ("Wrong email or password."), in about the same time (an e-mail that does not exist still runs one dummy hash). A correct password for a user who is not active gets `403 JV_FORBIDDEN` with `details.reason = "inactive"`, which the screen shows as "Your account is not active". The login screen shows no credentials or hints. The prototype's hard-coded `admin/admin123` and `staff/staff123` (`authStore.ts:13-16`) are deleted from the code and from the build. *Test:* T-D12, T-D25, T-E01. *Source:* SEC-01; docs #10, #14 [N]; NODE-DESIGN 2.4, 2.5.
- **U2.** There is no sign-up: no route creates an account except the admin door (U3), and a request to a sign-up-looking path answers `404`. *Test:* T-D13. *Source:* NODE-DESIGN 2.8.
- **U3.** Accounts are made only by Harsh, through the **admin door**: routes under `/admin/v1/` (create a user with a random temporary password that is shown once, reset a password, deactivate, activate, list). They work only while the environment variable `JV_ADMIN_TOKEN_HASH` is set; they compare the bearer token with that hash in constant time; they send no CORS headers and refuse any request that carries an `Origin` header, so a web page can never call them; they are throttled (A13); and every call is audited with the actor `admin-cli` (D16). On his Mac it is one command (`npm run admin -- add-user`) that asks for the token, the e-mail, the name and the role and prints the temporary password; no SQL and no database password is needed on his Mac. The first owner is made the same way, and **the admin door is the only way to make an owner**. Whether the token stays switched on after the first owner exists, and whether the owner may make other owners, is O41 (default: off until needed; only the admin door makes owners). A lost password is reset by Harsh the same way; there is no e-mail reset in M1 (outbound mail is unverified and adds attack surface). *Test:* T-D27, T-E09. *Source:* review Addendum A.4 (staff create and reset); NODE-DESIGN 2.8.
- **U4.** Sessions are **server-side**. A `sessions` row (D24) holds the SHA-256 of a 32-byte random ticket that the browser carries in a cookie (U12); every call looks it up, so a session ends at once when a user is deactivated, changes password or logs out. Two limits are enforced on the server on every call: an **absolute lifetime of 14 days** and an **idle lifetime of 12 hours** (`last_seen_at`); a session past either is ended and the answer is `401 JV_SESSION_EXPIRED`. The screen's polling does not count as activity (`GET /v1/sync/*` and `POST /v1/sync/poll` never move `last_seen_at`); every other call does, and so does `POST /v1/auth/activity`, which the screen sends on a click or key press at most once a minute. The screen also logs itself out after 12 hours without a click or key press (C16). The 12-hour and 14-day figures are choices for Siddhi (O42, which replaces O10b), not facts from any document; OWASP's usual idle range is minutes, not hours [N]. This gives on every plan what Supabase offered only on Pro. *Test:* T-D26, T-E08. *Source:* docs #5 of draft 3, F17; NODE-DESIGN 2.3, 2.7.
- **U5.** After login, and at start-up, the screen calls `GET /v1/auth/me`; the role it returns decides which routes and menu items exist (1.3). The API decides what is allowed (section 3). *Test:* T-E01.
- **U6.** The screen has no login library. Its state changes only on login, logout, `me` and any `401` (`JV_UNAUTHENTICATED`, `JV_SESSION_EXPIRED`): a `401` from any call, a poll included, empties memory, stops the pollers and shows the login screen once, without a loop. *Test:* T-E01 (login and logout repeated 20 times without a hang). *Source:* NODE-DESIGN 2.9.
- **U7.** One module-level gate in the application layout shows, in order: a loading screen, the login screen, then the app. No page reads the session on its own: today `app/page.tsx:17-23` reads it in a mount effect that never updates after the layout's login, so the Owner sees no rate card right after logging in (AC2). Pages read the role from a `useSession` hook. *Test:* T-E01. *Source:* hazard 3, AC2.
- **U8.** Logout calls `POST /v1/auth/logout` (the session is ended and the cookie cleared), empties every in-memory cache and stops every poller. After logout no business data remains in memory or in browser storage. *Test:* T-E05. *Source:* SEC-08.
- **U9.** Passwords are at least 10 and at most 128 characters; a short built-in list of very common passwords, and any password that contains the e-mail's local part, are refused. There is no leaked-password lookup in M1 (it would send data to a third party; O29 is closed by this rule). A new user gets a temporary password from Harsh's command and must change it at the first login (U11). *Test:* T-D25. *Source:* NODE-DESIGN 2.4.

Siddhi, default: public with unguessable ids (O11)]` *Test:* T-D21. *Source:* docs #8, DAT-06; HOSTINGER-FACTS 3.

### 4.3 Audit, numbers and repeat-safety

- **L1.** Every insert and update on `users`, `settings`, `daily_rates`, `categories`, `subcategories`, `designs`, `skus`, `sku_private`, `customers`, `bills`, `bill_items`, `receipts` and `receipt_allocations` produces **exactly one** `audit_log` row (never two), in the same transaction as the change, with the session user, the role and the old and new row as JSON; an update that changes only `updated_at` produces none (D23); columns that must never be copied (`password_hash`, `token_hash`) are on an exclude list per table, because the owner reads this table and it goes into the backups. Logins, failed logins, password changes, admin-door calls and job runs are written as `event` rows (D16). **Mechanism, chosen by the Hostinger proof (O48, test 3):** (a) if `CREATE TRIGGER` works for the plan's database user, generated triggers write the rows, reading the actor from a session variable that the transaction wrapper sets at the start of each transaction and clears at the end (the variable lives on the pooled connection and survives a commit [S]); then a change typed by hand in phpMyAdmin and the import are recorded too, with no actor and the role `sql` or `import`; (b) otherwise the API writes the same rows in the same transaction, and a change typed by hand is not audited (O50). Never both for one table. Draft 3 used triggers; draft 4 does not depend on them. Every service function and every edit has a test that counts the changed rows and the audit rows and compares them, run against the mechanism in use. *Test:* T-D14. *Source:* CMP-09; NODE-DESIGN 8 item 4, D-Q2; PART-A-MARIADB-MAPPING.md D16.
- **L2.** No route writes, updates or deletes `audit_log`, not even the owner's, and the code contains no statement that does (the statement wrapper and a grep test, as for S7). Where triggers work (L1 (a)), `BEFORE UPDATE` and `BEFORE DELETE` triggers with `SIGNAL` on `audit_log` make it append-only for every user, the database owner included [S]; without them an `UPDATE` typed by hand could still change it (Delete and Drop are blocked by S7 either way). *Test:* T-D14.
- **L3.** Numbers come from the `sys_counters` row of the series. **The row is created first**, by one autocommit statement outside any transaction, `INSERT INTO sys_counters (series, last_value) VALUES (?, 0) ON DUPLICATE KEY UPDATE series = series`; then, in the **same transaction** as the row it numbers, the number is taken with `UPDATE sys_counters SET last_value = LAST_INSERT_ID(last_value + 1) WHERE series = ?` and `SELECT LAST_INSERT_ID()` (which is per connection and takes no second lock). The update takes a row lock, so two callers are serialised; a rolled-back transaction rolls the counter back, so there are no gaps and no repeats (an `AUTO_INCREMENT` leaves a gap after a rollback and is never used for a human number [S]). The first step matters: when a new series is created inside the transaction and its first user rolls back while two others wait, one of the two gets a deadlock (1213) [S, spike F3]; a series row that exists beforehand avoids it, which counts on 1 January for `bill:<PREFIX>:<YYYY>` and on first use of `opening` and `design:<CATEGORY PREFIX>` (`sku:<design_no>` is made inside X8, where nobody else can know the new design number). A number that already exists (an imported code, IM3) is skipped by taking the next one, at most 100 times, then `JV_INVALID_INPUT`. Series: `bill:<PREFIX>:<YYYY>`, `opening`, `design:<CATEGORY PREFIX>`, `sku:<design_no>`. Measured locally: gap-free under 25 percent injected failures and 5 percent killed connections, and 650 to 750 numbers a second through the one counter row; not measured over a network [S]. *Test:* T-C01, T-C16. *Source:* docs #7, BIL-01, INV-09, hazard 4; SPIKE (f) and section 8 (F3).
- **L4.** Formats as coded: a bill is `<prefix>-<YYYY>-<4 digits>` (`appStore.ts:154-159`, prefix from `settings.estimate.prefix`, default `JV`; after 9,999 the digits simply grow); a design is `<category prefix>-<5 digits>` (`designPadding`); a piece is `<design_no>-<3 digits>` (`skuPadding`); a barcode is `<barcodePrefix>-<sku_no>` (default `BC`). The year is the **Asia/Kolkata year when the bill is saved**, so a backdated bill still takes the current year's number; whether numbering should follow the financial year is a question for the CA (O31). Opening-balance bills are `OB-<4 digits>`. A number is never reused, even after archiving. The screens do **not** show a "next number" before saving (the prototype did, `BillingPOSClient.tsx:70`, `designStore.ts:176`); they show "Number assigned when saved". *Test:* T-C01, T-C03, T-F07. *Source:* AC6, F18.
- **L5.** Repeat-safety: every function that creates something takes the request header `Idempotency-Key` (a UUID; draft 3's `p_request_id`), generated once per function call on the screen (a new-customer flow calls X6 and then X1, each with its own key; a retry of one call reuses that call's key). Its **first statement** is `INSERT INTO sys_idempotency (request_id, fn, actor, args_hash, claim_token) ... ON DUPLICATE KEY UPDATE request_id = request_id`, where `args_hash` is a SHA-256 of a canonical JSON of the route name, the path ids, the body and the actor id with keys sorted, and `claim_token` is a fresh random value. The function then reads that row back `FOR UPDATE`: if the token is its own it is first and carries on, storing its result in that row **in the same transaction**; with mysql2's default flags `affectedRows` is 1 for a fresh insert and for a duplicate alike (docs #7), so the token, not the count, tells which. If the token is another request's (the insert first waits for any in-flight identical request to commit), the function returns the stored result with the same status and the header `Idempotent-Replay: true` when `fn`, `actor` and `args_hash` all match, and raises `JV_INVALID_INPUT` otherwise (the same key with a different cart is refused, not answered with the first result). A first request that rolls back leaves no row, so the waiting twin becomes first and runs. A double click, a retry after a dropped connection, a second tab or two identical requests in flight at once cannot save twice and never show a false error. A request that fails the session, role or validation checks never reaches the claim and so never uses up its key. A writing call without an `Idempotency-Key` is refused with `JV_INVALID_INPUT` (X7 and the ordinary edits of A5 need none). The database runs READ-COMMITTED (S11), because under REPEATABLE READ an old snapshot made the duplicate probe fail with error 1020 [S]. Measured locally: 8 identical requests at once gave one bill, one counter step and 7 replays; different keys do not wait for each other [S]. *Test:* T-C05, T-C14, T-C18, T-E06. *Source:* hazard 1, BIL-02, F5; SPIKE (c); NODE-DESIGN 4.6.
- **L6.** For rows of `sys_idempotency` older than 90 days the nightly job (I8) empties `result`; the row itself stays (about 150 bytes; S7 allows no delete). A repeat of a key older than 90 days is refused with `JV_INVALID_INPUT` ("this request is too old to repeat"); it is never run again. *Test:* T-C05.

## 5. The cache contract (how the screens talk to the API)

The six store modules (`appStore`, `designStore`, `orderStore`, `settingsStore`, `catalogStore`, `authStore`; 1,408 lines, imported by 15, 17, 1, 6, 6 and 11 component files) keep their exported names. Behind them, memory is filled from the API after login and kept fresh by polling. This is what lets about 15 files that write data change, instead of all 37. The prototype's prices, totals and ledger are not computed again in the stores; the saved figures come back from the API.

- **C1.** Reads stay synchronous from memory (`getDesigns()`, `getInvoices()`, `getCustomers()`, `getSettings()` ...). `getInvoices()` returns bills in the prototype's `Invoice` shape (camelCase, with `items`, `payments`, and the derived `amountReceived`, `pendingAmount`, `status`, `fineWeightPending`) so the screens that only read need no change. *Test:* T-E04.
- **C2.** A **loading gate** in the application layout (U7) renders no data page until the first full hydrate has finished for the signed-in role. It shows progress ("3 of 8 tables"). If hydrate fails it shows an error with a retry button. It never falls back to sample data or to an empty list. **Time budget:** for the M1 volumes (up to 5,000 pieces, 2,000 customers, 3,000 bills) the first hydrate finishes within 10 seconds on a typical 4G connection. The payload is estimated at 8 to 12 MB before compression and whether Hostinger's proxy compresses is unknown [U]; the budget is therefore a test on the staging app on Hostinger, not a promise (O48). *Test:* T-E01, T-E05, T-F03. *Source:* DAT-05, hazard 8, F3.
- **C3.** Hydrate and poll are the same call: `GET /v1/sync/{resource}?updated_after=&after_key=&limit=`, ordered by (`updated_at`, key) and **keyset-paged**, with `limit` at most 1,000 (a constant in the API, so there is no silent cut by the server). The reply is `{rows, next}`: `next` is present when the page was full and the client calls again with `updated_after = next.updated_at` and `after_key = next.key`; `next: null` ends the load. A hydrate is a poll that starts from the beginning, and the last row of the last page is the first poll cursor; a row changed during a hydrate moves to the end of the order and is received again, and the cache merges by id keeping the greater `updated_at`, so nothing is skipped. The key is `id` (`setting_key` for `settings`, `sku_id` for `sku_private`; `customer_balances` is keyed by the customer's id). The comparison is written `updated_at > ? OR (updated_at = ? AND key > ?)`, because whether MariaDB turns the row-value form `(a, b) > (x, y)` into an index range is unknown [U]; a test reads `EXPLAIN` for it (A7). Each page is one `SELECT` with an explicit column list for the caller's role. The role decides the set. **Owner:** all resources **except** `audit_log`, `check_runs` and `backup_runs`, which are read on demand and paged when the owner opens them. **Biller:** stock (without `sku_private`), customers, `bills` (the header: subtotal, discount, additional charges, round-off and notes, which the `Invoice` shape needs and `bill_summary` does not carry), `bill_summary`, `bill_items`, `receipts`, `receipt_allocations`, `customer_balances`, rates, settings, categories, users. **Staff:** stock, categories, the settings keys it needs, users. The ledger view `customer_ledger` is loaded **per customer when a customer card opens** (`GET /v1/customers/{id}/ledger`, keyset-paged by the order key of V3) and reloaded when that customer's `updated_at` changes; `daily_totals` is loaded for a date range when a report opens (`GET /v1/daily-totals?from=&to=`, at most 400 days). The owner's other reads are `GET /v1/audit-log` (newest first, at most 200 a page), `GET /v1/health/checks` and `GET /v1/dashboard/health` (the dashboard line of section 12 in one call). *Test:* T-F03 (2,500 pieces all load). *Source:* F3; NODE-DESIGN 4.2.
- **C4.** **Polling** (Realtime is not used). Each resource group keeps a **cursor**: the greatest (`updated_at`, key) pair it has fully received, taken from the data, never from the browser's clock. Every 8 seconds (a setting, 5 to 10), and at once after any write the screen made, one request `POST /v1/sync/poll` carries a cursor for each resource the role may read (if the role may not read one of them the whole call is `403 JV_FORBIDDEN`, with no partial answer; it does not move the session's idle clock, U4); each resource answers with rows after its cursor minus a **30-second overlap**, ordered by (`updated_at`, key) and **keyset-paged** in pages of 1,000. The cursor moves **only after every page of that poll has arrived**. Results are merged by `id`, keeping the row with the greater `updated_at`, compared as microsecond numbers parsed from the exact text. `bill_summary` and `customer_balances` expose the base row's `updated_at`, which the touches move whenever a payment or allocation changes (D23), so pending and balance figures refresh. A service function cannot hold a row longer than the overlap: it takes its contended locks (customer, pieces, the number counter last) before it stamps any row, the lock wait is 5 seconds, and the whole call has a 15-second deadline with a 10-second statement limit (S11, S12), so no row is stamped more than 25 seconds before its commit. **Bulk work outside the API (an import, a repair script) is not bounded by that**, so it ends as IM1 says: one statement that sets `updated_at` to the current time on every row it changed, just before `COMMIT`, each transaction under 20 seconds, and then a new value of the `settings` row `data_epoch`; a screen that sees `data_epoch` change hydrates again from the start. The server clock and the API's clock are assumed to agree within a few seconds (a hand edit in phpMyAdmin is stamped by the database clock, the API's rows by the API clock, S6). A hidden tab polls every 30 seconds and at once when it becomes visible. Nothing is deleted and archived rows stay readable (A4), so no row ever disappears from a poll. *Test:* T-E02, T-E07, T-C15. *Source:* docs #1, #5, F3, RF-2; NODE-DESIGN 4.2.
- **C5.** Two kinds of write:
  - **(a) Ordinary edits** (stock content columns, categories, customer content columns, settings, daily rates): memory is updated at once and the row is marked *pending*; one write is awaited with **only the changed fields** and the stale check of A5 (the exact `updated_at` text); a pending row ignores polled copies until the write finishes; on success the returned row replaces it, on failure memory is rolled back and the reason shown.
  - **(b) Functions** (every function of section 10 and the opening-balance function): **never optimistic.** The screen shows a saving state, calls the function with a fresh `Idempotency-Key`, applies the **rows the function returns**, and on failure shows the message for the error code. A piece never shows Sold, and a bill never shows saved, before the API says so. *Test:* T-C06, T-E06. *Source:* hazard 1, `BillingSummaryPanel.tsx:163-167`.
- **C6.** The Save button is disabled while a save is running. A retry of the same user action reuses the same `Idempotency-Key` (L5). *Test:* T-E06.
- **C7.** **Offline.** When a poll or a write fails because the network is unreachable, a banner says "Offline: changes cannot be saved", every Save control is disabled, reads continue from memory, and polling retries with back-off (8, 16, then 30 seconds). When the API answers with a gateway error or a reply that is not JSON (an idle app being started, H7), the banner says "Waking up…" instead, and the call is retried with back-off for up to 30 seconds (a money call with the same `Idempotency-Key`). One successful call clears the banner. *Test:* T-E03.
- **C8.** **Browser storage.** No business data is written to `localStorage`, `sessionStorage` or IndexedDB. The only thing the browser holds from the login is the session cookie, which JavaScript cannot read (U12). The old `jaishri.*` keys are never read or written by the new build (the opening-data import reads them once from the old build, IM7). *Test:* T-E05. *Source:* DAT-05, DAT-07, SEC-08.
- **C9.** Render-time reads and `useState(getX())` stay valid because data is in memory after the gate; the 16 files that re-read the stores every second keep doing so only against memory, and none of them calls the network. *Test:* T-E02. *Source:* hazard 8.
- **C10.** The static export means no page depends on server rendering: every page is a client component behind the gate and no page touches `window` during prerender (the prototype's `typeof window` guards stay). The prototype already builds as 16 static routes (docs #17). *Test:* T-D12 (the build produces every route).
- **C11.** The cache never makes up a number or an id, except a new design's id (P2); new rows, ids and numbers come back from the database. *Test:* T-C01. *Source:* hazard 4.
- **C12.** **One pricing file, two copies of that file.** The live total on the billing screen comes from `src/lib/pricing.ts`, an integer implementation of the pricing (money in paise, weight in milligrams; no floating point). The API runs the **same file, byte for byte**, as `src/domain/pricing.ts`, next to a file `PRICING-SOURCE.txt` that names the source commit and the SHA-256; a script `check:pricing` compares the two hashes, and the app's pricing unit tests also run against the API's copy. The source of truth stays in the app repository. Because the website and the API are deployed separately, the API returns the SHA-256 of its `pricing.ts` in `GET /v1/auth/me` (`pricing_sha256`), the website's build knows its own hash, and on a mismatch the screen shows a banner and disables Save on bills. The saved bill always shows the API's figures; if they differ from the screen's preview the screen shows the saved figures and a notice. `quote_bill` (X7) runs the pricing without saving, so a test can compare it with the browser's preview on random input. This is a stronger guarantee than draft 3's database-against-browser parity test, because the money is priced by the code the screen previews with; the independent check is an exact-decimal oracle (a BigInt oracle and the SQL `DECIMAL` version of the spike, which agreed on 2,546 random bills and 20,000 half-way cases [S]). *Test:* T-A14, T-A15. *Source:* hazard 5; NODE-DESIGN 1.4; SPIKE (b).
- **C13.** Every error code (S8) has a plain message; an unknown error shows "Could not save. Nothing was changed." and writes the detail to the console only. *Test:* T-E04 (the error map) and each function test.
- **C14.** Reports, dashboard figures and the customer card are computed from the **same selectors** over the derived views (section 11) and business dates (S5), so they cannot disagree. **Opening-balance bills are not sales**: they are excluded from sales figures and from the daily cash totals. *Test:* T-B15, T-B21. *Source:* LED-04, LED-07, LED-08, LED-10, F4.
- **C15.** In M1 these are not connected and not reachable: Order Book, the public catalogue and its admin screens, the sample Audit and Sales tabs, the Estimate screen's date editor and edit mode, and the Reports screen's date controls and export buttons (QA LED-09, O32). *Test:* T-E04. *Source:* reviewer, `PLAN.md` P3-T3.
- **C16.** The screen logs out by itself after 12 hours without a click or key press (U4). *Test:* T-E08.
- **C17.** After any write that touches money, the screen re-reads that customer's `bill_summary` rows and `customer_balances` row at once (the next poll would also bring them). *Test:* T-E02.
- **C18.** The API looks up the session and the user on every call (A10), so a user who is deactivated, or whose role changed, in the middle of a shift gets a `401` or the new role's limits at the next call, at most one poll interval later; the screen then logs out and clears memory (U6). Draft 3 read the user's own `profiles` row in every poll for this; the check now belongs to the API. *Test:* T-E01, T-D26. *Source:* RF-12.

## 6. Backups, restore, hosting, build and import

### 6.1 Backups and restore

- **B1.** The production database is the MariaDB database on Harsh's Hostinger Business plan; no database plan is bought. Hostinger makes a daily automatic backup of files and databases (Business and higher), which is a bonus and **not the plan**: its retention and a database-only restore are not documented [H][N], and a restore overwrites live data. Point-in-time recovery does not exist here, so up to 24 hours of entries can be lost in a disaster (O28). *Test:* none: a plan step, ticked in the go or no-go checklist (plan task P3-T8); the nightly dump (B2) is the plan. *Source:* docs #12, #13.
- **B2.** Every night two things go to an off-site bucket (O45; the default is a Cloudflare R2 bucket used only for this): (1) a **full export of the data made by the API's own Node code, without the photo `bytes`**, encrypted; and (2) a **photo copy**: each photo that has not been copied yet is uploaded once. A photo never changes (its id is its content, P3), so one copy is enough; an export that held 2 GB of photos, kept as 30 daily and 12 monthly copies, would be about 100 GB, far above R2's free 10 GB, and would make every restore drill heavy (`NODE-DESIGN.md` D-Q11, which reverses the database session's note that the dump holds the photos). **The export:** inside one `START TRANSACTION WITH CONSISTENT SNAPSHOT` under REPEATABLE READ, set for that job's own session (docs #5), the job reads every table in keyset pages and writes newline-delimited JSON (DECIMAL as text, the `bytes` column left out) plus each table's `SHOW CREATE TABLE`; it gzips the file and encrypts it (a fresh AES-256-GCM key per file, wrapped with an RSA public key held in an environment variable; **the private key is held by Harsh and by the owner separately and never on the server or in the repository**, O40); it uploads to `daily/` and, on the first run of each month, also to `monthly/`, and the photo copy goes to `photos/` with each photo's `sha256`. The read-only snapshot is the one place where a network call may happen while a transaction is open (S12). **Bucket locks** (retention rules by prefix) stop a compromised API from deleting or overwriting the backups: `daily/` 30 days, `monthly/` 400 days, `photos/` indefinitely; the job prunes `daily/` only after the lock has expired (R2 has no write-only token, docs #15). The job writes a `backup_runs` row after each step (`db_dump`, `photo_copy`). A restore tool on Harsh's Mac rebuilds an empty database from the export and the photo copy; the restore drill uses the same tool (B4). `mariadb-dump` on Hostinger is unconfirmed (section 0 (d)); a Node export works without it. ## 8. Pricing rules

Symbols for one bill line: `NW` customer net weight (g); `q` quantity; `r` metal rate or piece price; `l` labour rate; `p` piroi; `o` other charges. All amounts are exact decimals (S3); "round" means round half up to the stated number of decimals.

- **R1. Labour rate by sale type.** Three different chains exist in the prototype and each has its own place. (1) **At stock creation**, a blank semi-wholesale rate takes the wholesale rate, else the retail rate, and a blank wholesale rate takes the retail rate (`AddDesignClient.tsx:1124-1127`); in the schema the three rates are never blank. (2) **At billing**, the default `l` is the piece's `labour_retail`, `labour_semi` or `labour_wholesale` for the bill's sale type (as coded `BillingPOSClient.tsx:28-32`: Wholesale falls back to semi, then retail, then the old single `labour`; Semi-Wholesale to semi, wholesale, retail, then `labour`; this default is applied by the screen, and the API prices the labour rate it is sent). The line's `l` can be edited on the screen. (3) **For old data** (the import), a missing semi-wholesale rate is `round((retail + wholesale) / 2, 2)` (`designStore.ts:45`). *Test:* T-A02, T-A03, T-C16. *Source:* AC1, F21.
- **R2. Default metal rate.** For a By Weight piece the screen pre-fills `r` with the `daily_rates` rate for the piece's material and purity that has the latest `rate_date` not after the bill's date; for a Per Piece piece `r` is the piece price for the sale type (R1) (the code reads one settings rate per material, `BillingPOSClient.tsx:98-100`; the by-purity, by-date default is the fix for QA BIL-10). `r` is **always sent by the screen** and is required by X1; it can be edited on the line. If no daily rate exists for the material and purity (for example `Custom`, 20K or 925 with no row), the screen asks for one and X1 returns the warning `RATE_MISSING`; if the latest rate is dated before the bill's date it returns `RATE_STALE`. *Test:* T-A13, T-A17. *Source:* BIL-10, F14.
- **R3. Labour amount.** By Weight: `round(NW × l, 2)`. Per Piece: `0`, because the labour is inside the piece price (as coded, `BillingPOSClient.tsx:34-39`, shown as "Included in piece price", `BillingItemsTable.tsx:144`). *Test:* T-A01, T-A06.
- **R4. Fine due.** `NW × q` when the sale type is **Wholesale** and the material is **Silver** and the selling method is **By Weight**; otherwise 0. *Fix:* the prototype also applied it to Per Piece pieces, which set the metal amount to 0 and dropped the piece price (`BillingPOSClient.tsx:41-44`, QA BIL-06). Purity and wastage are not applied (kept as coded, O2). *Test:* T-A04, T-A05. *Source:* BIL-06, BIL-07.
- **R5. Metal amount.** If the line's fine due is greater than 0: `0` (the silver is settled later, SF1). Else By Weight: `round(NW × r, 2)`; Per Piece: `round(r × q, 2)`. *Test:* T-A01, T-A04, T-A06. *Source:* `BillingPOSClient.tsx:46-51`.
- **R6. Quantity.** An integer from 1 to 1,000. A By Weight piece must have `q = 1` (the screen shows "1" for them, `BillingItemsTable.tsx:99`; O13). The quantity is saved on the line. *Fix:* the prototype lost it on save and every edit cut the price to one piece (`BillingSummaryPanel.tsx:95-115`, QA BIL-05). *Test:* T-A06. *Source:* BIL-05.
- **R7. Line total.** `metal_amount + labour_amount + p + o`. Piroi and other charges are added once per line, not multiplied by `q` (as coded, `BillingPOSClient.tsx:53-55`; O9). **Per-line rounding to paise (R3, R5) is new:** the prototype keeps floating-point fractions until it rounds the grand total, and a simulation of 200,000 random bills found that this rule changes the grand total by ₹1 on about 0.8 percent of bills. It is kept because exact paise on every line is what the ledger, the invariants and any later GST invoice need; it is flagged for Harsh as a ruling (O22). *Test:* T-A01, T-A07, T-A19. *Source:* F8, AC4.
- **R8. Bill totals.** `subtotal = sum of line totals`. `discount >= 0` and `additional_charges >= 0`. `before_round = subtotal - discount + additional_charges`, and **`before_round` must be at least 0**: a discount that would make it negative is rejected (a discount equal to `subtotal + additional_charges` is allowed and gives grand total 0). GST is 0 (estimate, as coded). `grand_total = round(before_round, 0)`, always a whole rupee (as coded: `Math.round` is applied whether or not the round-off setting is on, `BillingSummaryPanel.tsx:149`). `round_off = grand_total - before_round` when `settings.estimate.roundOff` is on, else `0`; it can be negative (1,000.40 gives −0.40). The prototype shows the Round Off line only when the setting is on (O12). *Test:* T-A08, T-A10. *Source:* DAT-10, BIL-04, `appStore.ts:253-275`, F8.
- **R9. Sale type.** Choosing a customer sets the bill's sale type to the customer's type (as coded, `BillingPOSClient.tsx:158-161`); the biller may change it. The sale type is stored on the bill and never changes (no edit in M1). *Test:* T-A03.
- **R10. Pieces that may be billed.** `In Stock` pieces; `Reserved` and `On Approval` pieces only when the screen passes their ids in `p_confirm_held` after showing a warning; `Sold`, `Returned`, `Repair`, `Transferred`, `Missing`, `Damaged` and `Archived` pieces never. *Fix:* the prototype only blocked `Sold` (`BillingPOSClient.tsx:85`, QA BIL-11). *Test:* T-C08. *Source:* BIL-11, O7.
- **R11. Payment status** (derived, never stored): `Cancelled` if the bill is cancelled; else `Paid` when pending is 0 and fine pending is 0; `Partial` when received is greater than 0 or fine settled is greater than 0; else `Pending` (as coded, `appStore.ts:62-63`). *Test:* T-B02.
- **R12. One function.** These rules are implemented once, in `priceLine` and `priceBill` of `pricing.ts`. `quote_bill` (X7) and `create_bill` (X1) call the same code, so a quote and a saved bill cannot differ. The same file runs in the browser (C12), and it is tested against an independent exact-decimal oracle on 200 random inputs. *Test:* T-A14, T-A15. *Source:* hazard 5.
- **R13. Versions.** A bill stores `pricing_version` (1 for the rules in this section). A later change to any rule (the fine formula, the piroi unit, rounding) becomes version 2 with its own functions; old bills keep version 1, and `quote_bill`, the nightly re-pricing check (I6) and the tests take the version as a parameter. So a rule change never breaks the check on old bills. *Test:* T-A15 (one case per version). *Source:* F11.
- **R14. List prices.** For every line the server records the price the screen should have offered, **computed by the server and not taken from the client**: `list_metal_rate` (the daily rate by R2, or the piece price) and `list_labour_rate` (the piece's rate for the sale type; a Per Piece line is compared on the price only, because its `labour_rate` is unused, R3). A line sold below either, or a discount above 10 percent of the subtotal, is counted in the nightly report to the owner (I11); nothing is blocked (O26). *Test:* T-B24, T-A13. *Source:* F12.

Worked example (the QA harness's own figure): NW 35.42 g, rate 6,920, labour 353 per gram, piroi 2.15, Retail, By Weight: metal 245,106.40, labour 12,503.26, line total 257,611.81, subtotal 257,611.81, grand total **257,612** (QA T2: ₹2,57,612). *Test:* T-A01.

## 9. Bills, receipts, allocation and fine

### 9.1 Life cycle

- **K1.** A bill is created `active` and can only become `cancelled`, once, by `cancel_bill` or `reissue_bill` (owner). There is **no edit** in M1: the bill and its lines are read-only. A correction is a **re-issue** (X10): the wrong bill is cancelled and the corrected bill is saved in one step, and the money already received moves to the corrected bill. Editing a bill with its payments intact is M2 (section 17). *Test:* T-D08, T-B19. *Source:* BIL-09, DAT-02, DAT-03, CMP-09, `PLAN.md` M1 cut, F2.
- **K2.** Creating a bill marks every piece on it `Sold` and sets its `sold_bill_id`, in the same transaction, only if each piece is still billable (R10). Two billers saving the same piece at the same moment: exactly one succeeds, the other gets `JV_PIECE_UNAVAILABLE` naming the piece, and **no number is used and nothing else changes**. *Test:* T-C04, T-C06. *Source:* INV-01, BIL-02, docs #1, #5.
- **K3.** Cancelling a bill (X5, with no replacement, for example when the customer returns the goods): the bill becomes `cancelled` with reason, time and person; each of its pieces whose `sold_bill_id` is this bill returns to `In Stock`; the bill's allocations get `released_at`; the **receipts stay active** (the money was received), so the customer holds an advance, which Y6 applies to their other pending bills or leaves as credit. Handing the money back is X11. *Test:* T-B10, T-B20. *Source:* BIL-12.
- **K4.** A customer is never deleted. They can be archived only when their outstanding, fine outstanding, advance and fine advance are all zero. Their bills and receipts stay. *Test:* T-C02. *Source:* LED-03, DAT-01.
- **K5.** The walk-in customer exists once, is never archived and never holds a PAN. Its id is the fixed uuid `00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001` (name "Walk-in Customer", no mobile), written by the seed, so that the import and the tests can name it. It may carry dues like anyone else (as coded: the prototype used the text id `walk-in`, `BillingSummaryPanel.tsx:137`), and its dues count in the outstanding figures; because one row stands for many different people, a sale that is not fully paid to the walk-in is **allowed but flagged** (O6), cash at the limit and bills at the PAN threshold need a named customer (section 13). **The walk-in never holds an advance**, because one row stands for strangers whose money must not pay each other's bills: X2 for the walk-in must name the bill (`p_bill_id`, so a payment never goes to another stranger's older bill) and takes no more than that bill's pending (`JV_OVERPAYMENT`), X5 refuses a walk-in bill that has live allocations with `JV_NOT_ALLOWED` (its receipts are voided first, with the reason, which shows the money going back on that day), X10 refuses a surplus over the new bill's total the same way, X12 refuses an advance for it, and I10 checks it. *Test:* T-B17, T-B23. *Source:* LED-07, F9, RM-3.
- **K6.** A re-issued bill points back to the one it replaces (`reissue_of`), a cancelled bill can be re-issued only once, and the customer cannot change in a re-issue; a second re-issue of the same bill meets a cancelled bill and raises `JV_BILL_CANCELLED`. *Test:* T-B19. *Source:* F2.

### 9.2 Receipts and allocation

- **Y1. Receipts.** Money received is a `receipts` row of kind `money` (mode Cash, UPI, Card, Bank Transfer or Other). Silver received is kind `fine_silver`. Silver settled in cash at a day's rate is kind `fine_cash` with `amount = round(grams × rate, 2)`; that amount **is money received** (cash totals include it) but it reduces fine, not rupees owed (as coded the cash was not booked at all, `appStore.ts:441-455`, QA LED-11). Money handed back is kind `refund` (Y10). Whether silver settled in cash counts as a sale or a receipt for tax is a question for Siddhi's CA `[CA]` (O2). *Test:* T-B12, T-B20. *Source:* LED-11, CMP-06, DAT-14.
- **Y2. "Credit" is not a payment.** A bill left partly or fully unpaid simply has no receipt for the rest. The mode `Credit` does not exist (D13). *Test:* T-B01. *Source:* BIL-03.
- **Y3. Oldest first.** To apply an amount (or fine grams) to bills, take the customer's `active` bills with something pending, ordered by `bill_date`, then `created_at`, then `id`. If the caller names a bill, it must belong to the customer, be active and have something pending (otherwise `JV_INVALID_INPUT`), and it goes first. Each bill takes the smaller of what remains and what is pending. "Something pending" means money pending for a money payment (X2) and silver pending for a fine settlement (X3); a payment row of amount 0 (the screen's default row) is ignored; a `fine_cash` settlement whose amount `round(grams × rate, 2)` is 0.00 raises `JV_INVALID_INPUT`. *Fix:* the prototype's "Apply to oldest outstanding" applied the payment to nothing (`recordPayment` with no invoice id, `appStore.ts:350-397`, QA LED-04). *Test:* T-B04, T-B05, T-B22. *Source:* LED-04, DAT-08.
- **Y4. Same customer only.** An allocation can only join a receipt and a bill of the same customer, enforced by composite foreign keys (D14) and again in the functions. *Test:* T-B06. *Source:* LED-01.
- **Y5. Overpayment.** At bill creation, payments totalling more than the bill's grand total are rejected (`JV_OVERPAYMENT`). In `record_payment`, any amount beyond what the customer's bills need is kept as an **advance** (money received that is neither allocated to a bill nor handed back) and the result says so; it is never lost. *Test:* T-B03, T-B04. *Source:* LED-06.
- **Y6. Advance and rebalance.** After any function that changes a customer's bills or receipts, the customer's **advance** is applied to their oldest pending bills, oldest receipt first (by `paid_on`, then `created_at`, then `id`), and unallocated silver grams the same way. **A rebalance applies in total at most the customer's `advance` of V2** (money received − live allocations − refunds), so money that was handed back is never spent a second time; opening advances are money received like any other. This **never changes an existing allocation**. Afterwards a customer has an advance only if they have no pending bill (invariant I10). Applying an advance to a new bill is shown on the billing screen as "Advance adjusted: ₹X" (O23). *Test:* T-B07, T-B14, T-B20, T-B22, T-B26. *Source:* review Q5, F1, RM-1.
- **Y7. Void, not delete.** `void_receipt` (owner) marks a receipt `voided` with a reason; its allocations stop counting; the receipt and the void stay in the ledger. Balances are derived, so a void restores exactly what the receipt had applied. A void shows in the ledger and in the daily totals **on the day it is done**, as a reversing line (V3, V4), so a closed day's cash total never changes because of it; the owner may void a receipt of an earlier day (a back-dated receipt or refund by the owner, Y8, does change an earlier day: O3). A void is refused with `JV_NOT_ALLOWED` ("void the refund first") when the customer's advance after it would be below 0, which happens only when the money was already handed back (X4). *Fix:* the prototype deleted the entry and could lose fine silver (`appStore.ts:462-518`, QA LED-02). *Test:* T-B08, T-B09. *Source:* LED-02, F2.
- **Y8. Dates.** `paid_on` and `bill_date` default to today (Asia/Kolkata). A biller can only use today; the owner can use any date up to and including today; a future date is rejected. A bill's date cannot be changed afterwards (the prototype let it be re-dated with no log, `updateInvoiceDate`). *Test:* T-B16. *Source:* CMP-09, LED-08.
- **Y9. Rebalance covers bills of every kind**, including `opening_balance` bills, which are the oldest by construction (D15). *Test:* T-B05, T-B21.
- **Y10. Refund.** `issue_refund` (owner, X11) records money handed back: kind `refund`, an amount greater than 0 and not more than the customer's current advance, a mode, and a date by Y8. It has no allocations, reduces the advance, appears in the ledger as a debit and in the daily totals as money paid out on its own date. Voiding a refund (X4) restores the advance. How a refund of money received against an estimate is treated for tax is a question for Siddhi's CA `[CA]` (O24). *Test:* T-B20. *Source:* F2.

### 9.3 Silver fine

- **SF1.** Fine is the silver weight owed from wholesale silver By Weight lines (R4). It is settled later by `settle_fine` in silver (`fine_silver`) or in cash at that day's silver rate (`fine_cash`, rate required and greater than 0). *Test:* T-B11, T-B13. *Source:* `appStore.ts:399-460`.
- **SF2.** Grams are applied oldest first (Y3), the named bill first. The grams must be greater than 0 and **not more than the customer's fine outstanding**; otherwise `JV_FINE_EXCEEDS` with the maximum allowed. *Fix:* the prototype capped silently and lost the extra silver (QA LED-12). *Test:* T-B11. *Source:* LED-12.
- **SF3.** Fine is not adjusted for purity, touch or wastage (as coded, QA BIL-07, LED-13, CMP-06). `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: how is fine worked out on Siddhi's bills? Siddhi and her CA, default: net weight, as coded]` (O2). A change becomes pricing version 2 (R13). *Test:* T-A04. *Source:* BIL-07.

- **X0. Lock order and timeouts (every function that writes).**
  1. First statement: the idempotency insert (L5).
  2. The caller's role check.
  3. **The customer row `FOR UPDATE`.** Every function that reads or changes a customer's bills, receipts or allocations takes this lock before it reads anything: X1, X2, X3, X4, X5, X10, X11, X12. X4, X5 and X10 find the customer by reading the receipt or the (old) bill first without a lock, then lock the customer, then re-read that row `FOR UPDATE` and check it is unchanged (if it changed, the call rolls back and answers `JV_BUSY`, and nothing was changed). This makes all money work for one customer run one at a time, so two concurrent bills or a bill beside a payment can never both apply the same advance (the review's case A and B). The shared walk-in customer serialises anonymous sales, which is acceptable at shop volume.
  4. Nothing further is locked for the customer's bills, receipts and allocations: the customer lock already lets one writer in at a time (a rebalance cannot lock rows in id order and does not need to).
  5. The pieces by id ascending (X1, X5, X10, X8, X9), taken by one `SELECT ... WHERE id IN (...) ORDER BY id FOR UPDATE` so that InnoDB locks them in primary-key order [S]. X8 and X9 take no customer lock.
  6. **The number counter row last**, after every check (overpayment, cash limit, available pieces) so the counter lock is held for the shortest time and cannot take part in a cycle.
  7. Every connection is set up as S11 says. `innodb_lock_wait_timeout = 5` makes a lock wait longer than 5 seconds fail with error 1205, which the API turns into `ROLLBACK` of the whole transaction and `JV_BUSY` (a 1205 rolls back only the failing statement, S12); the limit applies to every statement of the connection, so it also covers the idempotency insert of item 1. `max_statement_time = 10` cuts one statement (error 1969, also `JV_BUSY`), and the **deadline of 15 seconds** of S12 bounds the whole call, because `max_statement_time` is per statement and does not add up [S]. A deadlock (1213) is re-run by the API up to 2 more times (S12). The screen retries `JV_BUSY` once with the same Idempotency-Key (S8). *Test:* T-C10 to T-C13, T-C15, T-C19. *Source:* docs #1, #2, F1, RF-2, RM-6.
  8. **Test hook.** Each money function (X1 to X5, X10 to X12; the stock functions have no customer state) calls `ctx.pause('<function>.after_read')` once, after it has read the customer's state and before it allocates, and it allocates from what it read before the hook (a re-read after the hook would hide a missing lock). The hook **exists only in the test build**: the production build does not contain it (T-D29 greps the production bundle), so no request can reach it. For a two-session test the test passes each call a gate it controls (a promise that the test releases): there is **one gate per session**, session 1 is held at the hook and session 2 is released first, and the test runs in both release orders. No database object has to be replaced, committed and restored (draft 3's `create or replace` of `private.test_pause` and its polling of `pg_try_advisory_lock` disappear). A plain wait on the customer row would not prove the lock, because the foreign key of the bill insert may take its own lock on the same row (the documentation fetched says only that foreign-key checks take no gap locks at READ COMMITTED, docs #5). Each of these tests also has a variant in which the customer-lock helper is replaced by a no-op in the test build, and the same test must then fail. *Test:* T-C09 to T-C14. *Source:* RM-8, RM3-2; NODE-DESIGN 5.8.

## 11. Derived views

The four derived views are defined once, as MariaDB views or as queries in the API's read layer (a plan choice), and the nightly check I4 uses the same definitions. The API reads them with the caller's field allow-list and refuses them to staff and to callers with no session (A4); the owner and the biller can read them. Nothing in them is stored (S3). Each is read in pages of 1,000 in a total order, so paging is stable (C3). V1 and V2 expose the base row's `updated_at`, which the touches move whenever a payment or an allocation changes (D23, C4). A `SQL SECURITY INVOKER` view with window functions (V3) can be created by the application's database user [S].

Two words used below. A **live allocation** is a `receipt_allocations` row whose `released_at` is null and whose receipt is `active`. Money allocations come from `money` receipts and carry `amount`; fine allocations come from `fine_silver` and `fine_cash` receipts and carry `fine_grams` (their `amount` is 0). A `refund` receipt never has allocations.

- **V1. `bill_summary`**: one row per bill: `id`, `bill_no`, `customer_id`, `bill_date`, `doc_type`, `sale_type`, `status`, `reissue_of`, `grand_total`, `received` (the sum of live money allocations to the bill), `pending`, `fine_due` (`fine_weight_due`), `fine_settled` (the sum of live fine allocations), `fine_pending`, `payment_status` (R11), `item_count`, `created_at`, `updated_at`. For an active bill `pending = grand_total - received` and `fine_pending = fine_due - fine_settled`; for a cancelled bill `received`, `pending`, `fine_settled` and `fine_pending` are all 0 (K3 released its allocations). *Test:* T-B02, T-B10, T-B15.
- **V2. `customer_balances`**: one row per customer, the walk-in customer included, keyed by `id` (the customer's id, which the cache merges by). Rupees: `billed` (sum of `grand_total` of active bills, opening bills included), `total_purchases` (the same without `opening_balance` bills), `received` (sum of `amount` of active `money` receipts, opening advances included), `refunded` (sum of active `refund` receipts), `allocated` (sum of live money allocations), then `outstanding = billed - allocated`, `advance = received - allocated - refunded`, and `balance = billed - received + refunded`. Always `balance = outstanding - advance`; after Y6 at most one of `outstanding` and `advance` is above zero (I10), and then `outstanding = max(0, balance)` and `advance = max(0, -balance)`. Silver: `fine_billed` (sum of `fine_weight_due` of active bills), `fine_received` (sum of `fine_grams` of active fine receipts), `fine_allocated`, `fine_outstanding = fine_billed - fine_allocated`, `fine_advance = fine_received - fine_allocated`, `fine_balance = fine_billed - fine_received`. Also `last_purchase` (the latest `bill_date` of an active, non-opening bill) and the customer row's `updated_at`. `outstanding` and `advance` come from allocations while `balance` comes from totals; the two routes must agree (I4, I10). *Test:* T-B05, T-B15, T-B17, T-B20, T-B21.
- **V3. `customer_ledger`**: one row per event of a customer, with a running `balance` and `fine_balance` (debit minus credit, summed in event order, `partition by customer_id`, so a filter on one customer is applied before the sums). Events: `Invoice` for each bill, cancelled ones too (`Opening` for an `opening_balance` bill): dated `bill_date`, debit `grand_total`, fine debit `fine_weight_due`. `Cancellation` for each cancelled bill: dated the day it was cancelled, credit `grand_total`, fine credit `fine_weight_due`. `Payment` for each `money` receipt (`Opening advance` when `is_opening`): dated `paid_on`, credit `amount`. `Fine` for each `fine_silver` or `fine_cash` receipt: dated `paid_on`, fine credit `fine_grams`, no rupee entry (for `fine_cash` the description says "settled in cash at rate X = amount Y"). `Refund` for each `refund` receipt: dated `paid_on`, debit `amount`. `Void` for each voided receipt: dated the day of the void, the exact reverse of its own row. **The order key** is (`entry_date`, `entry_at`, `kind_rank`, `id`): `entry_at` is the row's `created_at` (`cancelled_at` or `voided_at` for the last two kinds); `created_at` of bills and receipts is set from the API's clock, which never returns the same instant twice (S5), so rows made in one transaction already differ; `kind_rank` makes the order total when two rows still tie (Invoice 1, Cancellation 2, Payment and Fine 3, Refund 4, Void 5), and `id` is the last tie-break. After the last row, `balance` equals V2's `balance` and `fine_balance` equals V2's `fine_balance` (I4). *Test:* T-B14, T-B25. *Source:* LED-04, LED-10, F18.
- **V4. `daily_totals`**: one row per (`business_date`, `mode`) for the modes Cash, UPI, Card, Bank Transfer and Other, with `money_in`, `money_out` and `net = money_in - money_out`. A `money` or `fine_cash` receipt adds `money_in` on its `paid_on` (so the Cash row includes silver settled in cash, Y1). A `refund` adds `money_out` on its `paid_on`. A voided receipt adds the **reverse** amount on the day of the void (a voided payment adds `money_out`, a voided refund adds `money_in`; an `is_opening` receipt, which V4 never counted, adds nothing), so a void never changes a day already closed (a back-dated receipt or refund by the owner, Y8, does: O3). Opening advances (`is_opening`) are not money received today and are left out (D15). The owner's evening check is three figures: today's Cash `net`, today's UPI `net`, and the sum of V2 `outstanding`. *Test:* T-B15, T-B16, T-B20, T-B21. *Source:* reviewer risk 1, F4.
- **V5. One set of selectors.** The dashboard, the reports and the customer card compute their figures from V1 to V4 through a single browser module, `src/lib/selectors.ts` (C14). **Sales** for a business date or month are the sum of `grand_total` of V1 rows with `status = 'active'` and `doc_type <> 'opening_balance'`, grouped by `bill_date`; cancelled bills are shown on their own line (count and value; a cancelled `opening_balance` bill is not counted there, because an opening-balance bill is never a sale, C14) and are not sales; a cancelled bill therefore leaves the day it was billed on (sales are a report, and only the cash book of V4 is kept stable for closed days). The module has a unit test over a fixed data set that checks every figure. *Test:* T-B15. *Source:* LED-10, DAT-08.

## 12. Invariants and the nightly check

`runInvariants()` (SQL statements run by the API; I6 and I11 are completed in Node because they re-price with `pricing.ts`) returns the rows that break each rule as (`invariant`, `row_ref`, `detail`); an empty result is a pass. It only reads. It has one snapshot of the data for its whole run: the job sets `REPEATABLE READ` for its own session and then starts `START TRANSACTION WITH CONSISTENT SNAPSHOT` (docs #5; at READ COMMITTED every statement would read its own snapshot [S]), so a payment saved while it runs cannot cause a false alarm.

- **I1.** Every `Sold` piece has a `sold_bill_id` of an `active` bill that has a line for it, and every line of an active bill points to a `Sold` piece with that bill id. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18.
- **I2.** No piece is on the lines of two active bills. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18.
- **I3.** For every active bill, live money allocations never exceed `grand_total` and live fine allocations never exceed `fine_weight_due`; for every active receipt, its live allocations never exceed its `amount` (money) or its `fine_grams` (fine); no allocation that is not released points to a cancelled bill; a fine allocation has `amount` 0 and a money allocation has `fine_grams` 0. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18.
- **I4.** For every customer, `balance` and `fine_balance` of V2 equal an independent recomputation from the base tables (`billed - received + refunded`, and the same for silver) and equal the last running balances in V3. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18.
- **I5.** Bill numbers are unique and, within each series (prefix and year, and `OB-`), the numbers issued by a function (those with no `import_batch_id`) are contiguous from the series' `seed_value + 1` (`seed_value` is 0 for a new series and is set by `seedCounters()` after an import, IM3), so an `OB-` number made by X12 after the import does not break the check; `seedCounters()` only raises a `seed_value`, and numbers at or below it are ignored. *Test:* T-B18, T-C16. *Source:* RF-6, RF3-5.
- **I6.** For every bill that has lines (opening bills have none, IM4): each stored line amount (`metal_amount`, `labour_amount`, `fine_weight_due`, `item_total`) equals a fresh run of `priceLine` of `pricing.ts` for the bill's `pricing_version` over the stored snapshot (R13); `subtotal` equals the sum of the lines and `fine_weight_due` the sum of the lines' fine due; `grand_total` equals `round(subtotal - discount + additional_charges, 0)`; and `round_off` is either 0 or `grand_total - (subtotal - discount + additional_charges)`, whichever the setting at the time of saving gave (so changing the setting later cannot fail the check). *Test:* T-B18, T-A15, T-C16.
- **I7.** No negative amount, weight or quantity exists in any table (except `round_off`, D11), no receipt has the mode `Credit`, no photo value starts with `data:`, and every `kind`, `mode` and `status` combination is one the checks of D13 allow (a guard in case a constraint is lost in a later migration). *Test:* T-B18.
- **I8. Nightly job.** One **hourly tick** drives all scheduled work. An hPanel cron job calls it every hour, and a scheduled GitHub Actions workflow calls it independently (it does nothing if the first call worked), either as a ten-line script of the app that calls `POST /internal/jobs/tick` (Hostinger's cron has a Node.js type that runs a script of the app with the Node 20 binary, every minute at the shortest [H]) or with `curl` (the Custom type); whether the cron process sees the app's environment variables, and the script path prefix, are unknown [U], so both forms are specified and the Hostinger proof (O48, test 7) picks the one that works. The endpoint needs a bearer token (the environment holds only its hash), is throttled (A13), sends no CORS headers and refuses an `Origin` header. **The tick is idempotent and decides what is due**, from the business date in Asia/Kolkata and the rows of `sys_job_runs`, `check_runs` and `backup_runs`: the nightly check if none has run for today's date and it is after 02:00 (computed in the API); the export and the photo copy of B2 if none for today; the housekeeping of L6. So no time-zone assumption about cron is made, a late or missed hour costs nothing, a `running` row older than 15 minutes counts as failed and may be run again, and a second call the same day answers "already done". The tick also keeps the app warm (H7). It runs synchronously with its own 130-second limit and without the 15-second call deadline of S12, because a background task would die silently if the process were stopped. The nightly check runs `runInvariants()` and writes a `check_runs` row (`ok`, and `details` with the count per invariant and at most 20 sample rows each). MariaDB's event scheduler cannot do this job: it is off on the plan, a normal user cannot switch it on, and an event created while it is off is accepted but never runs [H][S]. Nobody is paged in M1: the owner sees the dashboard line (below) whenever she opens it, and Harsh reads the check each morning for the first two weeks (plan task P4-T1); a tick that did not run turns the dashboard red after 26 hours. *Test:* T-B18, T-R04. *Source:* reviewer risk 1; PART-A-MARIADB-MAPPING.md section 2; NODE-DESIGN 7.3.
- **I9.** The same function runs inside the test suite after random sequences of operations (T-B14) and after deliberate damage (T-B18), so a rule that drifts fails in tests before it can fail at the counter. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18.
- **I10. The rebalance post-condition (Y6).** For every customer: the sum of V1 `pending` over their active bills equals V2 `outstanding`, and the sum of V1 `fine_pending` equals `fine_outstanding`; `outstanding`, `advance`, `fine_outstanding` and `fine_advance` are not negative; if `advance` is above zero then `outstanding` is 0, and if `fine_advance` is above zero then `fine_outstanding` is 0; and the walk-in customer's `advance` and `fine_advance` are 0 (K5). *Test:* T-B14, T-B18, T-B26, T-C10, T-C11. *Source:* F1 (case B).
- **I11. The review line (a report, not an invariant; it never turns the check red).** For the previous business day: the bill lines sold below the server's list prices (R14), the bills with a discount above the constant `discount_flag_percent` (10) percent of the subtotal, the bills and receipts saved with a cash override reason (Z1), the cancellations, re-issues, voids and refunds, the walk-in's cash total and number of bills of the day (Z2 does not add them up, so this is where a split sale shows), the `fine_cash` settlements whose rate is more than the constant `rate_flag_percent` (5) percent away from every Silver rate of that day (the latest rate dated not after it, per purity; no Silver rate at all counts as away), the daily rates entered by a biller that moved more than that percent from the previous rate of the same material and purity, and the bills whose sale type differs from the customer's type (R9). The counts go into `check_runs.details`; the owner can open the lists (up to 50 rows each). Nothing is blocked (O26). *Test:* T-B24. *Source:* F12.
- **I12. Backup heartbeat (B2).** The dashboard line shows how many hours ago the last `backup_runs` row with `ok` for `db_dump`, and for `photo_copy`, was written, and the database size against the 2 GB red line (P5). *Test:* T-R03. *Source:* F15.
- **I13. Cash book tie-out.** For each mode, the sum of V4 `net` over all dates equals the sum of the active `money` and `fine_cash` receipts of that mode that are not `is_opening`, minus the active refunds of that mode. It guards the void-day logic of V4. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18. *Source:* RM-13.

It turns **red** when the last check says Problem, when the last `check_runs` row is older than 26 hours (the job stopped), when the last good dump or photo copy is older than 26 hours, or when the database is above 2 GB; it turns amber when the database user has a Delete or Drop right on (S11). *Test:* T-B18, T-R03.

**Mutation tests (T-B18).** For each of I1 to I7, I10 and I13 a test damages exactly one row with a direct SQL statement on the local test database and expects `runInvariants()` to name that invariant and that row; with no damage it returns nothing. ## 13. Cash limit and PAN prompt

Not legal or tax advice. The figures, the comparison (at or above, or above) and the legal posture are for Siddhi's CA `[CA]`; the reading used here comes from secondary sources only (research row 19, open item (j) of section 0). The thresholds are constants in the API's constants file `limits` (`cash_limit = 200000`, `pan_threshold = 200000`, `discount_flag_percent = 10`, `rate_flag_percent = 5`) so a decision changes one number in one file.

- **Z1. Cash from a named customer.** For a customer that is not the walk-in, let `cash_today` be the sum of `amount` of their active receipts with `paid_on` on one business date that are `money` receipts of mode `Cash` or `fine_cash` receipts, leaving out `is_opening` rows and not reduced by refunds. When a function (X1 for the cash payment rows of a new bill, X10 through the rules of X1, X2 for a `Cash` payment, X3 for `fine_cash`) would make `cash_today` **reach `cash_limit` or more**, it raises `JV_CASH_LIMIT`, unless the caller is the **owner** and passes `p_cash_override_reason` of at least 10 characters. A biller cannot override: the owner enters that payment. The reason is saved on the bill (`cash_override_reason`) or on the receipt (`cash_override_reason`) and audited (L1, I11). Only the per-day total is built. Not built: the limb for one transaction paid in several parts, possibly over several days (which a per-day sum does not see), and the limb for one event or occasion (O14). *Test:* T-A12, T-B23. *Source:* CMP-08, F9.
- **Z2. The walk-in customer.** The walk-in row stands for many different people, so its cash is never added up over a day. Instead: a single cash payment (one `record_payment`, one `fine_cash` settlement, or the cash rows of one bill) of `cash_limit` or more for the walk-in raises `JV_CASH_LIMIT` with the message "Select or create the customer"; and X1 refuses a bill whose `grand_total` is `pan_threshold` or more for the walk-in with `JV_NOT_ALLOWED` and the same message (the PAN rule says "exceeding"; the stricter reading is deliberate and O14 asks the CA). There is no override for the walk-in: the owner creates the customer first. The walk-in never holds a PAN (D10). *Test:* T-A12, T-B23. *Source:* K5, F9.
- **Z3. PAN prompt.** When a bill's `grand_total` is `pan_threshold` or more and the named customer has no `pan`, X1 still saves the bill and returns the warning `PAN_MISSING`; the screen asks for the PAN and saves it on the customer (a content column, A5). A PAN is ten characters: five capital letters, four digits, one capital letter; anything else is refused by the `CHECK` on the customer row (D10), and the screen validates before it sends. It is a prompt, not a block, because a buyer may legitimately have no PAN and give a declaration instead. *Test:* T-A12. *Source:* CMP-08.
- **Z4.** Neither rule creates a new printable document (Harsh's ruling on "documents"); a buyer's declaration, if one is needed, is kept on paper outside the system `[CA]`. *Test:* none. *Source:* Harsh's ruling of 5 October.

## 17. Out of scope, and room left for M2

**Out of scope here:** see 1.1. **Room left, so M2 needs no breaking migration:**

- **Edit a bill (BIL-08, BIL-09, DAT-02, DAT-03).** An `amend_bill` function replaces the lines with the same pricing code (R12), keeps every receipt and allocation, re-derives balances (they are derived), and keeps the old version in a `bill_revisions` table. **18.10.5 Where the reviewer should look first (the lines the writer is least sure of)**

1. **S12 (3), S11 and C4**: the whole-call bound. `max_statement_time` is per statement; fractions are accepted [W], so a stricter rule (each statement gets the time left of the 15 seconds) is possible and is a plan choice; the claim "no row is stamped more than 25 seconds before its commit" is reasoned, not run. The reviewed design keeps 15 + 15 (row 7 above).
2. **S7, L6, U4, D24, P3, O51**: no `DELETE` anywhere departs from the reviewed design (row 2). Check that nothing in the spec still needs a delete, that the growth estimate is acceptable, and that keeping an unproven trigger or a second database out of the plan is the right call.
3. **L1, O50**: the audit rule is "exactly one row, mechanism by the proof". Does CMP-09 ("records edited with no log") still count as closed when, without triggers, a hand edit in phpMyAdmin is not audited? Are the hash-column exclusions complete (`password_hash`, `token_hash`)?
4. **P1 to P7, D26, B2, I12, O47**: Harsh's photo numbers come from `DB-handover.md`, not from the design; upload first with no foreign key, insert-only rows and the `removed_at` tombstone that empties `bytes` differ from the schema draft (row 14); the public read by id with a cross-origin resource policy [U]; the export without photo bytes plus a once-only photo copy (row 3) brings `photo_copy` and its heartbeat back.
5. **H1, O39, O38, A12**: the website stays on Vercel (Hobby, risk accepted by Harsh) and the domain is a new one (Harsh's later decisions); the CSP must name the API origin in `connect-src` and `img-src`.
6. **I8, T-R04, O48**: the hourly tick; two forms of the cron start; whether the cron process sees the environment variables is unknown; the tick has no 15-second deadline.
7. **H7, C7, T-E03, T-C18**: the on-demand start of the app, "Waking up…", and how long a cold start takes are all untested [U].
8. **A13**: only the photo-read figure is the writer's own; the rest are the design's. The client address behind Hostinger's proxy is unknown.
9. **U1**: Argon2id needs Node 24 (24.7 or newer) on the plan, while Hostinger's cron uses Node 20; the scrypt fallback numbers and the 2-hash limit are from `NODE-DESIGN.md` and OWASP [N], not run.
10. **L5**: the claim-token read-back is the spike's pattern described in prose; the exact SQL sequence was not copied from the spike's helper.
11. **S5, S6**: the API clock that never repeats an instant, explicit stamps plus column defaults, and the assumption that the API and database clocks agree within seconds (C4).
12. **D10, D14, D19**: the `walkin_key` generated column is from the spike; the need for `UNIQUE (id, customer_id)` under the composite foreign keys was measured by the writer [W]; the extra internal-table indexes of D19 are the writer's own and were not run.

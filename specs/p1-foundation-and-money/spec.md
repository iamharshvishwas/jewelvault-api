## 1. Scope, roles and conventions

### 1.1 Scope

- **In scope (milestone M1, billing live):** login with roles; stock (designs and pieces, owner-only weight); customers; bills (read-only; correction by re-issue); payments, silver-fine settlements, refunds and void; derived balances and the ledger view; opening balances and the import hooks; daily rates and the stale-rate warning; photos in storage; audit log; backups and restore; nightly checks; the cache contract between the screens and the database. **The refund function (X11) is the first thing to leave M1 if the calendar slips** (until then a same-day mistake is a void).
- **Forward-compatible, not built in M1:** edit a bill without losing payments, Order Book, public catalogue, stock search, tags, Excel upload, new document types, partial returns. The schema in Part A leaves room for them (section 17) so they need no breaking migration.
- **Out of scope for this spec:** tax invoices and bills of supply (decision 4), partial returns and credit notes, old-gold purchase, karigar ledger, Hindi interface, privacy-law screens (DPDP, due about 13 May 2027, 18 months after the Rules of 13 November 2025 as QA T7 reads them; a shorter period was proposed but none is notified), e-way bills, the in-app staff screen, the printed layout (task P3-T1).

### 1.2 Words

- **Bill** is what the prototype calls an "estimate" or "invoice" (`Invoice` in `mockData.ts`). It is not a GST tax invoice. `[CA]`
- **Piece** (SKU) is one physical item with a unique code. **Design** groups pieces that look alike.
- **NW** is `customerNetWeight`, the weight used for billing. **Original net weight** is the hidden measured weight (`originalNetWeight`).
- **Fine** is the silver weight owed on a wholesale silver bill, settled later in silver or in cash at that day's silver rate.
- **Piroi** is an amount added per line. As coded it is added as rupees (`BillingPOSClient.tsx:54`). Whether it is rupees or grams is open (O1).
- **Receipt** is money received, silver received, or money handed back (a refund).
- **Advance** is money (or silver) a customer has paid that is not applied to any bill; it is derived, never stored (V2).
- **Re-issue** is cancelling a bill and issuing a corrected one in one step, keeping the money received (X10).
- **Opening balance** is what a customer owed (or had paid in advance) on the day the shop moves to this system (D15).
- **Owner, Biller, Staff, anon** are the four kinds of caller (1.3).

### 1.4 Conventions (apply to every table and function)

- **S1.** Schemas: `public` holds the tables (all with row-level security), the read views and the callable functions; `private` holds helpers, number counters, the idempotency log, audit functions and the nightly check, and is **not** exposed through the API. The API's exposed schemas are `public` only (`api.schemas = ["public"]`; the default also lists `storage` and `graphql_public`, docs #6). *Test:* T-D01, T-D17. *Source:* docs #2, #6; F6.
- **S2.** Identifiers are `uuid` (`gen_random_uuid()`), never a clock or a count. A design's id may be generated by the screen before the design exists (P2); every other id and every human number is made by the database (L3). *Test:* T-C01, T-C03. *Source:* hazard 4, BIL-01, INV-09.
- **S3.** Money is `numeric(14,2)` (rupees and paise). Weight is `numeric(12,3)` (grams to the milligram). A rate per gram is `numeric(12,2)`. A count is `integer`. No `real`, `double precision` or `float` column or variable exists in any money path. *Test:* T-A11, T-F02. *Source:* hazard 5.
- **S4.** Sanity caps are enforced by `CHECK` constraints and again inside the functions, **on inputs and on computed amounts**: a weight is at most 100,000 g; a rate or a piece price is at most 100,000,000; a quantity is 1 to 1,000; a bill has 1 to 200 lines; every line amount (metal amount, labour amount, piroi and other charges), line total, subtotal and grand total, and every payment, refund, discount, additional charge, opening balance and `fine_cash` amount (grams × rate), is at most 1,000,000,000. A value over a cap raises `JV_INVALID_INPUT`, never a database overflow error. An input with more decimals than its column (more than 2 on a rate or an amount, more than 3 on a weight) raises `JV_INVALID_INPUT` too, because it would otherwise be priced unrounded and stored rounded; the screens round before sending. A trailing zero counts, as the value was sent (`scale()` of the number): `1.230` is refused as a rate and accepted as a weight, and the screens never send one. Negative values are rejected everywhere (except `round_off`, D11). The caps are written as literals in the `CHECK` constraints (a `CHECK` that called a function in `private` would fail for the role doing the update, because that role may not execute it; tested) and also in `private.limits`, which the functions read; test T-F02 reads every `CHECK` from the catalogue and compares it with `private.limits`, so the two cannot drift. A `CHECK`, a default, a generated column, a policy or a view calls built-in functions only (a policy that called `private.today()` would fail every insert for a role that may not execute it). *Test:* T-A09, T-A16, T-A18, T-F02. *Source:* BIL-04, INV-03, DAT-10, F19, RF-4, RM-11, RM-12.
- **S5.** The business time zone is `Asia/Kolkata`. A bill or receipt carries a calendar `date` (the business day) and a `timestamptz` (when it was saved). "Today" is `(private.now_ts() at time zone 'Asia/Kolkata')::date`, and every function takes the time from `private.now_ts()` (it returns `clock_timestamp()`), so a test can fix the clock by replacing that one function inside its own rolled-back transaction; the seam is unreachable from the API. Reports group by the `date` columns, never by a UTC timestamp. *Test:* T-B16, T-F07. *Source:* LED-08, DAT-11, RM-15.
- **S6.** Every table has `created_at`, `updated_at` and, where a person acts, `created_by`, except the three append-only logs `audit_log`, `check_runs` and `backup_runs`, which have the one time column that D16 and D17 give them (`at`, `ran_at`). `updated_at` is set by a trigger on every update and uses `clock_timestamp()` (not `now()`, which is the start of the transaction); `created_at` and the insert value of `updated_at` default to `clock_timestamp()` too (a default calls built-in functions only, S4); the trigger `private.set_updated_at` then stamps both from `private.now_ts()` (S5) on every insert and update and overrides the defaults, so a test that fakes the clock fakes every stamp of a row, not only its dates. The columns that name a person (`created_by`, `updated_by`, `set_by`, `confirmed_by`, `voided_by`, `cancelled_by`) are **always set by a trigger or function from `auth.uid()`**; a value sent by a client is ignored. The audit trigger ignores an update that changes only `updated_at` (D23). *Test:* T-F02, T-D22. *Source:* F7.
- **S7.** No API role (`anon`, `authenticated`) has `DELETE` on any business table in `public`. Rows are archived, voided or cancelled, never removed. Only the database owner (Harsh, through the SQL editor, in an emergency) can delete. Photo files are the one exception and are governed by P3. *Test:* T-D07, T-C02. *Source:* LED-03, DAT-01, INV-09.
- **S8.** Errors from the functions are raised with `RAISE EXCEPTION` and a stable code at the start of the message: `JV_FORBIDDEN`, `JV_INVALID_INPUT`, `JV_NOT_FOUND`, `JV_PIECE_UNAVAILABLE`, `JV_CUSTOMER_ARCHIVED`, `JV_OVERPAYMENT`, `JV_CASH_LIMIT`, `JV_FINE_EXCEEDS`, `JV_BILL_CANCELLED`, `JV_ALREADY_VOID`, `JV_NOT_ALLOWED`, `JV_BUSY`. The screens map each code to a plain message. A lock timeout (55P03) is caught inside each function and raised as `JV_BUSY`. Other database errors (a unique violation 23505, a deadlock 40P01) reach the screen as a generic "Could not save. Nothing was changed."; for `JV_BUSY` and 40P01 the screen retries **once automatically with the same `p_request_id`**, which is safe because of L5. *Test:* T-C04, T-C15 and each function test. *Source:* docs #7.
- **S9.** Migrations are forward-only SQL files in `supabase/migrations/`, one concern per file, each re-runnable on an empty database. A migration that adds a table enables row-level security in the same file (A1). *Test:* T-F04. *Source:* docs #2.
- **S10.** A helper that policies call is named `private.app_role()` (not `current_role`, which is an SQL keyword). *Test:* T-D16. *Source:* F20.

# PART A: FOUNDATION

## 2. Data model

All tables are in `public` unless marked `private`. "FK" means a foreign key with `ON DELETE RESTRICT` (nothing is ever deleted, S7). Column names are given in the database's `snake_case`; the screens keep their `camelCase` fields through the cache layer (C1).

### 2.1 Reference and settings

- **D1. `profiles`**: `id uuid` primary key and FK to `auth.users` with `ON DELETE RESTRICT` (a login that has a profile cannot be deleted: the person columns of the other tables are plain uuids, so deleting the profile would erase the only record of who that person was; retire a person with `active = false`, and to remove a login made by mistake the database owner deletes its profile row first); `full_name text not null`; `role text not null` (`owner`, `biller`, `staff`); `active boolean not null default false`. A trigger on `auth.users` inserts a profile (`role = 'staff'`, `active = false`, `full_name` from the login's metadata or, when there is none, the part of the email before the `@`) for every new login, so a new login can do nothing until it is activated (U3). Every active user may read every profile row (so the screens can show who created or changed something); only the owner may update one (A8). *Test:* T-D06, T-E01. *Source:* SEC-02, SEC-04, review "created by names".
- **D2. Helper `private.app_role()`**: `security definer`, `stable`, `search_path = ''`; returns the caller's role when their profile is active, else `null`. Every policy calls it as `(select private.app_role())` so Postgres runs it once per statement. It is the only `private` function `authenticated` may execute (A3). *Test:* T-D06, T-D16. *Source:* docs #4.
- **D3. `settings`**: `key text primary key`, `value jsonb not null`, `updated_by uuid`. One row per key, never one blob. Keys: `business` (legal and display name, location, owner name, phone, address, GSTIN, tagline), `estimate` (number prefix, round-off flag, footer note), `taxes`, `tag_template`, `numbering` (design and piece padding, barcode prefix, category prefixes), `materials` (names and purities), `locations` (the four stock locations of `EditStockClient.tsx:13`: Main Showroom, Jaishri Vault, Workshop, Exhibition), `custom_fields`, `data_epoch` (a timestamp text that bulk database work changes, C4). The prototype's `users` list moves to `profiles`; its `defaultGoldRate` and `defaultSilverRate` move to `daily_rates`. *Test:* T-F02. *Source:* `settingsStore.ts:36-82`, hazard 7, AC3.
- **D4. `daily_rates`**: `id`, `material` (`Gold`/`Silver`), `purity text`, `rate_per_gram numeric(12,2)` (greater than 0), `rate_date date`, `confirmed_by uuid` (forced, S6); unique on (`rate_date`, `material`, `purity`). The rate a bill screen pre-fills for a material and purity is the row with the latest `rate_date` not after the bill's date; if there is none, the screen asks for one (X1 returns the warning `RATE_MISSING`); if the latest one is older than the bill's date it is still pre-filled and the screen shows a warning (X1 returns `RATE_STALE`, and the bill still saves, T-A17). The owner may add a rate for any date up to today and correct a row dated today (an audited update); a biller may add a rate dated today, once per material and purity (a policy `WITH CHECK (rate_date = (clock_timestamp() at time zone 'Asia/Kolkata')::date)`, written inline for the reason in S4). A purity with no rate row (for example `Custom`) needs a typed rate (O27). *Test:* T-A13, T-A17, T-D05. *Source:* BIL-10, CMP-05, `settingsStore.ts:109`, `DashboardRateCard.tsx:25`, F7, F14.
- **D5. `categories`** (`name` unique ignoring case, `prefix` 2 to 6 capitals or digits, `active`) and **`subcategories`** (`category_id` FK, `name`, unique per category ignoring case, `active`). Seeded from `DEFAULT_CATEGORIES`, `DEFAULT_CATEGORY_PREFIXES` and `DEFAULT_SUBCATEGORY_LIST` (`catalogStore.ts`). *Test:* T-F06. *Source:* `catalogStore.ts`.

### 2.2 Stock

- **D6. `designs`**: `id`; `design_no text not null` (unique ignoring case; from a counter for new designs, L3; her own code when imported, IM1); `name text`; `category_id` FK; `subcategory_id` FK null; **`material`, `purity` and `selling_method` live here and only here**; `material` is `Gold` or `Silver` (a `CHECK`: the prototype's type, `daily_rates` and the pricing rules R4 and R5 know only those two, and the import writes designs straight into the table); `description`, `notes`, `tags text[]`; `primary_image_path`, `image_paths text[]`; `archived_at`; `import_batch_id`. ## 3. Access rules

- **A1.** Row-level security is on for **every** table in `public` and `private`, enforced twice: each migration enables it (S9) and an event trigger enables it on any table created later in `public` or `private` (the example on the event-triggers page, docs #4, names only `public`; the trigger here covers both). A check query fails the build if any such table has it off. *Test:* T-D01. *Source:* lint 0013, docs #2.
- **A2.** Roles come from `profiles` through `private.app_role()`. No policy reads `auth.jwt()` metadata, `user_metadata` or `app_metadata`. *Test:* T-D06, T-D15. *Source:* lint 0015, SEC-02.
- **A3.** **Default grants are removed, in this order, in the first migration** (the exact effect was tested on the Supabase image on 14 October): (1) `alter default privileges for role postgres revoke execute on functions from public;` in its **global** form with no `in schema`, because the four statements on the securing-your-api page (docs #2) are all written `in schema public` and, for functions, the built-in grant to `public` survives them, so a later function in `public` or `private` would stay executable by `anon` and `authenticated`; then, in schema `public`, `alter default privileges for role postgres in schema public revoke all on tables`, `on sequences` and `on functions from anon, authenticated`; (2) for objects that already exist: `revoke all` on every table and sequence in `public` from `anon` and `authenticated`, and on every function in `public` from `public`, `anon` and `authenticated`; (3) `revoke all on schema private from public; grant usage on schema private to authenticated;` and `revoke all` on every object in `private` from `public`, `anon` and `authenticated`, then **only** `grant execute on function private.app_role() to authenticated`; (4) each grant of A4 and A6, explicitly. `service_role` keeps its default access (the platform's own services use it; no service key reaches the browser). `anon` has no `USAGE` on `private`. `api.schemas` is `["public"]` and the unused GraphQL extension is disabled. *Test:* T-D02, T-D16, T-D17. *Source:* docs #2, #6, F6, RF-3.
- **A4.** The table and view matrix (S = select, I = insert, U = update; "content columns" are listed in A5). **All read roles may SELECT archived rows** (the screens hide them); this is required because an update that reads columns also applies the SELECT policy (docs #10):

### 4.3 Audit, numbers and repeat-safety

- **L1.** An audit trigger (`security definer`, `search_path = ''`) writes one `audit_log` row for every insert and update on: `profiles`, `settings`, `daily_rates`, `categories`, `subcategories`, `designs`, `skus`, `sku_private`, `customers`, `bills`, `bill_items`, `receipts`, `receipt_allocations`. It records `auth.uid()`, the role and the old and new row as JSON, and skips an update that changes only `updated_at` (D23). *Test:* T-D14. *Source:* CMP-09.
- **L2.** No API role can write, update or delete `audit_log`, not even the owner. *Test:* T-D14.
- **L3.** Numbers come from `private.next_number(series)`, which runs `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (series) DO UPDATE SET last_value = counters.last_value + 1 RETURNING last_value` in the **same transaction** as the row it numbers. The conflict update takes a row lock, so two callers are serialised; a rolled-back transaction rolls the counter back, so there are no gaps and no repeats. A number that already exists (an imported code, IM3) is skipped by taking the next one, at most 100 times, then `JV_INVALID_INPUT`. Series: `bill:<PREFIX>:<YYYY>`, `opening`, `design:<CATEGORY PREFIX>`, `sku:<design_no>`. *Test:* T-C01, T-C16. *Source:* docs #8, BIL-01, INV-09, hazard 4.
- **L4.** Formats as coded: a bill is `<prefix>-<YYYY>-<4 digits>` (`appStore.ts:154-159`, prefix from `settings.estimate.prefix`, default `JV`; after 9,999 the digits simply grow); a design is `<category prefix>-<5 digits>` (`designPadding`); a piece is `<design_no>-<3 digits>` (`skuPadding`); a barcode is `<barcodePrefix>-<sku_no>` (default `BC`). The year is the **Asia/Kolkata year when the bill is saved**, so a backdated bill still takes the current year's number; whether numbering should follow the financial year is a question for the CA (O31). Opening-balance bills are `OB-<4 digits>`. A number is never reused, even after archiving. The screens do **not** show a "next number" before saving (the prototype did, `BillingPOSClient.tsx:70`, `designStore.ts:176`); they show "Number assigned when saved". *Test:* T-C01, T-C03, T-F07. *Source:* AC6, F18.
- **L5.** Repeat-safety: every function that creates something takes `p_request_id uuid`, generated once per function call on the screen (a new-customer flow calls X6 and then X1, each with its own id; a retry of one call reuses that call's id). Its **first statement** is `INSERT INTO private.idempotency (request_id, fn, actor, args_hash) ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, where `args_hash` is a hash of the arguments. If the row was inserted, the function carries on and stores its result in that row **in the same transaction**. If it was not inserted (the insert first waits for any in-flight identical request to commit), the function returns the stored result when `fn`, `actor` and `args_hash` all match, and raises `JV_INVALID_INPUT` otherwise (the same id with a different cart is refused, not answered with the first result). A double click, a retry after a dropped connection, a second tab or two identical requests in flight at once cannot save twice and never show a false error. *Test:* T-C05, T-C14, T-E06. *Source:* hazard 1, BIL-02, F5.
- **L6.** Rows of `private.idempotency` older than 90 days are deleted by the nightly job (I8). *Test:* T-C05.

## 5. The cache contract (how the screens talk to the database)

The six store modules (`appStore`, `designStore`, `orderStore`, `settingsStore`, `catalogStore`, `authStore`; 1,408 lines, imported by 15, 17, 1, 6, 6 and 11 component files) keep their exported names. Behind them, memory is filled from the database after login and kept fresh by polling. This is what lets about 15 files that write data change, instead of all 37.

- **C1.** Reads stay synchronous from memory (`getDesigns()`, `getInvoices()`, `getCustomers()`, `getSettings()` ...). `getInvoices()` returns bills in the prototype's `Invoice` shape (camelCase, with `items`, `payments`, and the derived `amountReceived`, `pendingAmount`, `status`, `fineWeightPending`) so the screens that only read need no change. *Test:* T-E04.
- **C2.** A **loading gate** in the application layout (U7) renders no data page until the first full hydrate has finished for the signed-in role. It shows progress ("3 of 8 tables"). If hydrate fails it shows an error with a retry button. It never falls back to sample data or to an empty list. **Time budget:** for the M1 volumes (up to 5,000 pieces, 2,000 customers, 3,000 bills) the first hydrate finishes within 10 seconds on a typical 4G connection. *Test:* T-E01, T-E05, T-F03. *Source:* DAT-05, hazard 8, F3.
- **C3.** Hydrate reads each table in **pages of 1,000 rows** with `.range(from, to)` and a stable order by the primary key (`id`; `key` for `settings`, `sku_id` for `sku_private`), **each next page starting after the rows already received** (not at a fixed +1,000) and the load ending on an empty page, so a lowered `max_rows` costs requests, not rows (with `max_rows = 500` a loop that stepped by 1,000 loaded 1,500 of 2,500 rows without an error); the build check asserts `api.max_rows = 1000` in the repository (H3) and DB21 reads the hosted value. The API returns at most 1,000 rows per call for tables, views and functions (docs #6), so a single call would silently cut the data. The role decides the set. **Owner:** all tables **except** `audit_log`, `check_runs` and `backup_runs`, which are read on demand and paged when the owner opens them. **Biller:** stock (without `sku_private`), customers, `bill_summary`, `bill_items`, `receipts`, `receipt_allocations`, `customer_balances`, rates, settings, categories, profiles. **Staff:** stock, categories, the settings keys it needs, profiles. The ledger view `customer_ledger` is loaded **per customer when a customer card opens** and reloaded when that customer's `updated_at` changes; `daily_totals` is loaded for a date range when a report opens. *Test:* T-F03 (2,500 pieces all load). *Source:* F3.
- **C4.** **Polling** (Realtime is not used). Each table group keeps a **cursor**: the greatest (`updated_at`, `id`) pair it has fully received, taken from the data, never from the browser's clock. Every 8 seconds (a setting, 5 to 10), and at once after any write the screen made, a poll asks for rows after the cursor minus a **30-second overlap**, ordered by (`updated_at`, `id`) and **keyset-paged** in pages of 1,000. The cursor moves **only after every page of that poll has arrived**. Results are merged by `id`, keeping the row with the greater `updated_at`, compared as microsecond numbers parsed from the text. `bill_summary` and `customer_balances` expose the base row's `updated_at`, which the touch triggers move whenever a payment or allocation changes (D23), so pending and balance figures refresh. A function writer cannot hold a row longer than the overlap: each function carries `SET lock_timeout = '5s'` (X0 item 7) and takes its contended locks (customer, pieces, the number counter last) before it stamps any row, so the time between a stamp and the commit is CPU time, not lock time; the whole call is also bounded by the **role's** `statement_timeout`, set in a migration with `alter role authenticated set statement_timeout = '15s'` and `notify pgrst, 'reload config'` (the platform ships 8 seconds; a `set local statement_timeout` inside a function does not shorten the call already running, tested on the Supabase image on 14 October). **Bulk work by the database owner (an import, a repair script) is not bounded by that**, so it ends as IM1 says: one statement that sets `updated_at = clock_timestamp()` on every row it changed, just before `COMMIT`, each transaction under 20 seconds, and then a new value of the `settings` row `data_epoch`; a screen that sees `data_epoch` change hydrates again from the start. A hidden tab polls every 30 seconds and at once when it becomes visible. Nothing is deleted and archived rows stay readable (A4), so no row ever disappears from a poll. *Test:* T-E02, T-E07, T-C15. *Source:* docs #8, F3, RF-2.
- **C5.** Two kinds of write:
  - **(a) Ordinary edits** (stock content columns, categories, customer content columns, settings, daily rates): memory is updated at once and the row is marked *pending*; one write is awaited with **only the changed fields** and the stale check of A5 (the exact `updated_at` text); a pending row ignores polled copies until the write finishes; on success the returned row replaces it, on failure memory is rolled back and the reason shown.
  - **(b) Functions** (every function of section 10 and the opening-balance function): **never optimistic.** The screen shows a saving state, calls the function with a fresh `p_request_id`, applies the **rows the function returns**, and on failure shows the message for the error code. A piece never shows Sold, and a bill never shows saved, before the database says so. *Test:* T-C06, T-E06. *Source:* hazard 1, `BillingSummaryPanel.tsx:163-167`.
- **C6.** The Save button is disabled while a save is running. A retry of the same user action reuses the same `p_request_id` (L5). *Test:* T-E06.
- **C7.** **Offline.** When a poll or a write fails because the network is unreachable, a banner says "Offline: changes cannot be saved", every Save control is disabled, reads continue from memory, and polling retries with back-off (8, 16, then 30 seconds). One successful call clears the banner. *Test:* T-E03.
- **C8.** **Browser storage.** No business data is written to `localStorage`, `sessionStorage` or IndexedDB. Only the Supabase Auth session is stored, by the library. The old `jaishri.*` keys are never read or written by the new build (the opening-data import reads them once from the old build, IM7). *Test:* T-E05. *Source:* DAT-05, DAT-07, SEC-08.
- **C9.** Render-time reads and `useState(getX())` stay valid because data is in memory after the gate; the 16 files that re-read the stores every second keep doing so only against memory, and none of them calls the network. *Test:* T-E02. *Source:* hazard 8.
- **C10.** The static export means no page depends on server rendering: every page is a client component behind the gate and no page touches `window` during prerender (the prototype's `typeof window` guards stay). The prototype already builds as 16 static routes (docs #17). *Test:* T-D12 (the build produces every route).
- **C11.** The cache never makes up a number or an id, except a new design's id (P2); new rows, ids and numbers come back from the database. *Test:* T-C01. *Source:* hazard 4.
- **C12.** **One pricing function, two copies.** The live total on the billing screen comes from `src/lib/pricing.ts`, an integer copy of the database's pricing (money in paise, weight in milligrams; no floating point). The saved bill always shows the database's figures; if they differ the screen shows the saved figures and a notice. A read-only database function `quote_bill` (X7) runs the database pricing without saving, so a test can compare the two on random input. *Test:* T-A14, T-A15. *Source:* hazard 5.
- **C13.** Every error code (S8) has a plain message; an unknown error shows "Could not save. Nothing was changed." and writes the detail to the console only. *Test:* T-E04 (the error map) and each function test.
- **C14.** Reports, dashboard figures and the customer card are computed from the **same selectors** over the derived views (section 11) and business dates (S5), so they cannot disagree. **Opening-balance bills are not sales**: they are excluded from sales figures and from the daily cash totals. *Test:* T-B15, T-B21. *Source:* LED-04, LED-07, LED-08, LED-10, F4.
- **C15.** In M1 these are not connected and not reachable: Order Book, the public catalogue and its admin screens, the sample Audit and Sales tabs, the Estimate screen's date editor and edit mode, and the Reports screen's date controls and export buttons (QA LED-09, O32). *Test:* T-E04. *Source:* reviewer, `PLAN.md` P3-T3.
- **C16.** The screen logs out by itself after 12 hours without a click or key press (U4). *Test:* T-E08.
- **C17.** After any write that touches money, the screen re-reads that customer's `bill_summary` rows and `customer_balances` row at once (the next poll would also bring them). *Test:* T-E02.
- **C18.** Each poll also reads the signed-in user's own `profiles` row by id; if it is gone, inactive or its role changed, the screen logs out and clears memory (a user deactivated in the middle of a shift would otherwise get empty poll results instead of errors and keep every cached customer and bill on screen). *Test:* T-E01. *Source:* RF-12.

## 8. Pricing rules

Symbols for one bill line: `NW` customer net weight (g); `q` quantity; `r` metal rate or piece price; `l` labour rate; `p` piroi; `o` other charges. All amounts are exact decimals (S3); "round" means round half up to the stated number of decimals.

- **R1. Labour rate by sale type.** Three different chains exist in the prototype and each has its own place. (1) **At stock creation**, a blank semi-wholesale rate takes the wholesale rate, else the retail rate, and a blank wholesale rate takes the retail rate (`AddDesignClient.tsx:1124-1127`); in the new schema the three rates are never blank. (2) **At billing**, the default `l` is the piece's `labour_retail`, `labour_semi` or `labour_wholesale` for the bill's sale type (as coded `BillingPOSClient.tsx:28-32`: Wholesale falls back to semi, then retail, then the old single `labour`; Semi-Wholesale to semi, wholesale, retail, then `labour`; this default is applied by the screen, and the database prices the labour rate it is sent). The line's `l` can be edited on the screen. (3) **For old data** (the import), a missing semi-wholesale rate is `round((retail + wholesale) / 2, 2)` (`designStore.ts:45`). *Test:* T-A02, T-A03, T-C16. *Source:* AC1, F21.
- **R2. Default metal rate.** For a By Weight piece the screen pre-fills `r` with the `daily_rates` rate for the piece's material and purity that has the latest `rate_date` not after the bill's date; for a Per Piece piece `r` is the piece price for the sale type (R1) (the code reads one settings rate per material, `BillingPOSClient.tsx:98-100`; the by-purity, by-date default is the fix for QA BIL-10). `r` is **always sent by the screen** and is required by X1; it can be edited on the line. If no daily rate exists for the material and purity (for example `Custom`, 20K or 925 with no row), the screen asks for one and X1 returns the warning `RATE_MISSING`; if the latest rate is dated before the bill's date it returns `RATE_STALE`. *Test:* T-A13, T-A17. *Source:* BIL-10, F14.
- **R3. Labour amount.** By Weight: `round(NW × l, 2)`. Per Piece: `0`, because the labour is inside the piece price (as coded, `BillingPOSClient.tsx:34-39`, shown as "Included in piece price", `BillingItemsTable.tsx:144`). *Test:* T-A01, T-A06.
- **R4. Fine due.** `NW × q` when the sale type is **Wholesale** and the material is **Silver** and the selling method is **By Weight**; otherwise 0. *Fix:* the prototype also applied it to Per Piece pieces, which set the metal amount to 0 and dropped the piece price (`BillingPOSClient.tsx:41-44`, QA BIL-06). Purity and wastage are not applied (kept as coded, O2). *Test:* T-A04, T-A05. *Source:* BIL-06, BIL-07.
- **R5. Metal amount.** If the line's fine due is greater than 0: `0` (the silver is settled later, SF1). Else By Weight: `round(NW × r, 2)`; Per Piece: `round(r × q, 2)`. *Test:* T-A01, T-A04, T-A06. *Source:* `BillingPOSClient.tsx:46-51`.
- **R6. Quantity.** An integer from 1 to 1,000. A By Weight piece must have `q = 1` (the screen shows "1" for them, `BillingItemsTable.tsx:99`; O13). The quantity is saved on the line. *Fix:* the prototype lost it on save and every edit cut the price to one piece (`BillingSummaryPanel.tsx:95-115`, QA BIL-05). *Test:* T-A06. *Source:* BIL-05.
- **R7. Line total.** `metal_amount + labour_amount + p + o`. Piroi and other charges are added once per line, not multiplied by `q` (as coded, `BillingPOSClient.tsx:53-55`; O9). **Per-line rounding to paise (R3, R5) is new:** the prototype keeps floating-point fractions until it rounds the grand total, and a simulation of 200,000 random bills found that this rule changes the grand total by ₹1 on about 0.8 percent of bills. It is kept because exact paise on every line is what the ledger, the invariants and any later GST invoice need; it is flagged for Harsh as a ruling (O22). *Test:* T-A01, T-A07, T-A19. *Source:* F8, AC4.
- **R8. Bill totals.** `subtotal = sum of line totals`. `discount >= 0` and `additional_charges >= 0`. `before_round = subtotal - discount + additional_charges`, and **`before_round` must be at least 0**: a discount that would make it negative is rejected (a discount equal to `subtotal + additional_charges` is allowed and gives grand total 0). GST is 0 (estimate, as coded). `grand_total = round(before_round, 0)`, always a whole rupee (as coded: `Math.round` is applied whether or not the round-off setting is on, `BillingSummaryPanel.tsx:149`). `round_off = grand_total - before_round` when `settings.estimate.roundOff` is on, else `0`; it can be negative (1,000.40 gives −0.40). The prototype shows the Round Off line only when the setting is on (O12). *Test:* T-A08, T-A10. *Source:* DAT-10, BIL-04, `appStore.ts:253-275`, F8.
- **R9. Sale type.** Choosing a customer sets the bill's sale type to the customer's type (as coded, `BillingPOSClient.tsx:158-161`); the biller may change it. The sale type is stored on the bill and never changes (no edit in M1). *Test:* T-A03.
- **R10. Pieces that may be billed.** `In Stock` pieces; `Reserved` and `On Approval` pieces only when the screen passes their ids in `p_confirm_held` after showing a warning; `Sold`, `Returned`, `Repair`, `Transferred`, `Missing`, `Damaged` and `Archived` pieces never. *Fix:* the prototype only blocked `Sold` (`BillingPOSClient.tsx:85`, QA BIL-11). *Test:* T-C08. *Source:* BIL-11, O7.
- **R11. Payment status** (derived, never stored): `Cancelled` if the bill is cancelled; else `Paid` when pending is 0 and fine pending is 0; `Partial` when received is greater than 0 or fine settled is greater than 0; else `Pending` (as coded, `appStore.ts:62-63`). *Test:* T-B02.
- **R12. One function.** These rules are implemented once in `private.price_line` and `private.price_bill`. `quote_bill` (X7) and `create_bill` (X1) call the same code, so a quote and a saved bill cannot differ. The integer copy in `src/lib/pricing.ts` (C12) is tested against it on 200 random inputs. *Test:* T-A14, T-A15. *Source:* hazard 5.
- **R13. Versions.** A bill stores `pricing_version` (1 for the rules in this section). A later change to any rule (the fine formula, the piroi unit, rounding) becomes version 2 with its own functions; old bills keep version 1, and `quote_bill`, the nightly re-pricing check (I6) and the tests take the version as a parameter. So a rule change never breaks the check on old bills. *Test:* T-A15 (one case per version). *Source:* F11.
- **R14. List prices.** For every line the server records the price the screen should have offered, **computed by the server and not taken from the client**: `list_metal_rate` (the daily rate by R2, or the piece price) and `list_labour_rate` (the piece's rate for the sale type; a Per Piece line is compared on the price only, because its `labour_rate` is unused, R3). A line sold below either, or a discount above 10 percent of the subtotal, is counted in the nightly report to the owner (I11); nothing is blocked (O26). *Test:* T-B24, T-A13. *Source:* F12.

Worked example (the QA harness's own figure): NW 35.42 g, rate 6,920, labour 353 per gram, piroi 2.15, Retail, By Weight: metal 245,106.40, labour 12,503.26, line total 257,611.81, subtotal 257,611.81, grand total **257,612** (QA T2: ₹2,57,612). *Test:* T-A01.

## 9. Bills, receipts, allocation and fine

### 9.1 Life cycle

- **K1.** A bill is created `active` and can only become `cancelled`, once, by `cancel_bill` or `reissue_bill` (owner). There is **no edit** in M1: the bill and its lines are read-only. A correction is a **re-issue** (X10): the wrong bill is cancelled and the corrected bill is saved in one step, and the money already received moves to the corrected bill. Editing a bill with its payments intact is M2 (section 17). *Test:* T-D08, T-B19. *Source:* BIL-09, DAT-02, DAT-03, CMP-09, `PLAN.md` M1 cut, F2.
- **K2.** Creating a bill marks every piece on it `Sold` and sets its `sold_bill_id`, in the same transaction, only if each piece is still billable (R10). Two billers saving the same piece at the same moment: exactly one succeeds, the other gets `JV_PIECE_UNAVAILABLE` naming the piece, and **no number is used and nothing else changes**. *Test:* T-C04, T-C06. *Source:* INV-01, BIL-02, docs #8.
- **K3.** Cancelling a bill (X5, with no replacement, for example when the customer returns the goods): the bill becomes `cancelled` with reason, time and person; each of its pieces whose `sold_bill_id` is this bill returns to `In Stock`; the bill's allocations get `released_at`; the **receipts stay active** (the money was received), so the customer holds an advance, which Y6 applies to their other pending bills or leaves as credit. Handing the money back is X11. *Test:* T-B10, T-B20. *Source:* BIL-12.
- **K4.** A customer is never deleted. They can be archived only when their outstanding, fine outstanding, advance and fine advance are all zero. Their bills and receipts stay. *Test:* T-C02. *Source:* LED-03, DAT-01.
- **K5.** The walk-in customer exists once, is never archived and never holds a PAN. Its id is the fixed uuid `00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001` (name "Walk-in Customer", no mobile), written by the seed, so that the import and the tests can name it. It may carry dues like anyone else (as coded: the prototype used the text id `walk-in`, `BillingSummaryPanel.tsx:137`), and its dues count in the outstanding figures; because one row stands for many different people, a sale that is not fully paid to the walk-in is **allowed but flagged** (O6), cash at the limit and bills at the PAN threshold need a named customer (section 13). **The walk-in never holds an advance**, because one row stands for strangers whose money must not pay each other's bills: X2 for the walk-in must name the bill (`p_bill_id`, so a payment never goes to another stranger's older bill) and takes no more than that bill's pending (`JV_OVERPAYMENT`), X5 refuses a walk-in bill that has live allocations with `JV_NOT_ALLOWED` (its receipts are voided first, with the reason, which shows the money going back on that day), X10 refuses a surplus over the new bill's total the same way, X12 refuses an advance for it, and I10 checks it. *Test:* T-B17, T-B23. *Source:* LED-07, F9, RM-3.
- **K6.** A re-issued bill points back to the one it replaces (`reissue_of`), a cancelled bill can be re-issued only once, and the customer cannot change in a re-issue; a second re-issue of the same bill meets a cancelled bill and raises `JV_BILL_CANCELLED`. *Test:* T-B19. *Source:* F2.

### 9.2 Receipts and allocation

- **Y1. Receipts.** Money received is a `receipts` row of kind `money` (mode Cash, UPI, Card, Bank Transfer or Other). Silver received is kind `fine_silver`. Silver settled in cash at a day's rate is kind `fine_cash` with `amount = round(grams × rate, 2)`; that amount **is money received** (cash totals include it) but it reduces fine, not rupees owed (as coded the cash was not booked at all, `appStore.ts:441-455`, QA LED-11). Money handed back is kind `refund` (Y10). Whether silver settled in cash counts as a sale or a receipt for tax is a question for Siddhi's CA `[CA]` (O2). *Test:* T-B12, T-B20. *Source:* LED-11, CMP-06, DAT-14.
- **Y2. "Credit" is not a payment.** A bill left partly or fully unpaid simply has no receipt for the rest. The mode `Credit` does not exist (D13). *Test:* T-B01. *Source:* BIL-03.
- **Y3. Oldest first.** To apply an amount (or fine grams) to bills, take the customer's `active` bills with something pending, ordered by `bill_date`, then `created_at`, then `id`. If the caller names a bill, it must belong to the customer, be active and have something pending (otherwise `JV_INVALID_INPUT`), and it goes first. Each bill takes the smaller of what remains and what is pending. "Something pending" means money pending for a money payment (X2) and silver pending for a fine settlement (X3); a payment row of amount 0 (the screen's default row) is ignored; a `fine_cash` settlement whose amount `round(grams × rate, 2)` is 0.00 raises `JV_INVALID_INPUT`. *Fix:* the prototype's "Apply to oldest outstanding" applied the payment to nothing (`recordPayment` with no invoice id, `appStore.ts:350-397`, QA LED-04). *Test:* T-B04, T-B05, T-B22. *Source:* LED-04, DAT-08.
- **Y4. Same customer only.** An allocation can only join a receipt and a bill of the same customer, enforced by composite foreign keys (D14) and again in the functions. *Test:* T-B06. *Source:* LED-01.
- **Y5. Overpayment.** At bill creation, payments totalling more than the bill's grand total are rejected (`JV_OVERPAYMENT`). In `record_payment`, any amount beyond what the customer's bills need is kept as an **advance** (money received that is neither allocated to a bill nor handed back) and the result says so; it is never lost. *Test:* T-B03, T-B04. *Source:* LED-06.
- **Y6. Advance and rebalance.** After any function that changes a customer's bills or receipts, the customer's **advance** is applied to their oldest pending bills, oldest receipt first (by `paid_on`, then `created_at`, then `id`), and unallocated silver grams the same way. **A rebalance applies in total at most the customer's `advance` of V2** (money received − live allocations − refunds), so money that was handed back is never spent a second time; opening advances are money received like any other. This **never changes an existing allocation**. Afterwards a customer has an advance only if they have no pending bill (invariant I10). Applying an advance to a new bill is shown on the billing screen as "Advance adjusted: ₹X" (O23). *Test:* T-B07, T-B14, T-B20, T-B22, T-B26. *Source:* review Q5, F1, RM-1.
- **Y7. Void, not delete.** `void_receipt` (owner) marks a receipt `voided` with a reason; its allocations stop counting; the receipt and the void stay in the ledger. Balances are derived, so a void restores exactly what the receipt had applied. A void shows in the ledger and in the daily totals **on the day it is done**, as a reversing line (V3, V4), so a closed day's cash total never changes because of it; the owner may void a receipt of an earlier day (a back-dated receipt or refund by the owner, Y8, does change an earlier day: O3). A void is refused with `JV_NOT_ALLOWED` ("void the refund first") when the customer's advance after it would be below 0, which happens only when the money was already handed back (X4). *Fix:* the prototype deleted the entry and could lose fine silver (`appStore.ts:462-518`, QA LED-02). *Test:* T-B08, T-B09. *Source:* LED-02, F2.
- **Y8. Dates.** `paid_on` and `bill_date` default to today (Asia/Kolkata). A biller can only use today; the owner can use any date up to and including today; a future date is rejected. A bill's date cannot be changed afterwards (the prototype let it be re-dated with no log, `updateInvoiceDate`). *Test:* T-B16. *Source:* CMP-09, LED-08.
- **Y9. Rebalance covers bills of every kind**, including `opening_balance` bills, which are the oldest by construction (D15). *Test:* T-B05, T-B21.
- **Y10. Refund.** `issue_refund` (owner, X11) records money handed back: kind `refund`, an amount greater than 0 and not more than the customer's current advance, a mode, and a date by Y8. It has no allocations, reduces the advance, appears in the ledger as a debit and in the daily totals as money paid out on its own date. Voiding a refund (X4) restores the advance. How a refund of money received against an estimate is treated for tax is a question for Siddhi's CA `[CA]` (O24). *Test:* T-B20. *Source:* F2.

### 9.3 Silver fine

- **SF1.** Fine is the silver weight owed from wholesale silver By Weight lines (R4). It is settled later by `settle_fine` in silver (`fine_silver`) or in cash at that day's silver rate (`fine_cash`, rate required and greater than 0). *Test:* T-B11, T-B13. *Source:* `appStore.ts:399-460`.
- **SF2.** Grams are applied oldest first (Y3), the named bill first. The grams must be greater than 0 and **not more than the customer's fine outstanding**; otherwise `JV_FINE_EXCEEDS` with the maximum allowed. *Fix:* the prototype capped silently and lost the extra silver (QA LED-12). *Test:* T-B11. *Source:* LED-12.
- **SF3.** Fine is not adjusted for purity, touch or wastage (as coded, QA BIL-07, LED-13, CMP-06). `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: how is fine worked out on Siddhi's bills? Siddhi and her CA, default: net weight, as coded]` (O2). A change becomes pricing version 2 (R13). *Test:* T-A04. *Source:* BIL-07.

## 10. The functions

All callable functions are in `public`. Each is `security definer` with `set search_path = ''`, revokes execute from `public` and `anon`, grants it to `authenticated`, and begins with the role check (A6). Each function that writes takes `p_request_id uuid` (L5), writes inside one transaction, and returns `jsonb` holding every row it created or changed so the cache can apply them (C5(b)). All raise `JV_*` codes (S8).

- **X0. Lock order and timeouts (every function that writes).**
  1. First statement: the idempotency insert (L5).
  2. The caller's role check.
  3. **The customer row `FOR UPDATE`.** Every function that reads or changes a customer's bills, receipts or allocations takes this lock before it reads anything: X1, X2, X3, X4, X5, X10, X11, X12. X4, X5 and X10 find the customer by reading the receipt or the (old) bill first without a lock, then lock the customer, then re-read that row `FOR UPDATE` and check it is unchanged. This makes all money work for one customer run one at a time, so two concurrent bills or a bill beside a payment can never both apply the same advance (the review's case A and B). The shared walk-in customer serialises anonymous sales, which is acceptable at shop volume.
  4. Nothing further is locked for the customer's bills, receipts and allocations: the customer lock already lets one writer in at a time (a rebalance cannot lock rows in id order and does not need to).
  5. The pieces by id ascending (X1, X5, X10, X8, X9). X8 and X9 take no customer lock.
  6. **The number counter row last**, after every check (overpayment, cash limit, available pieces) so the counter lock is held for the shortest time and cannot take part in a cycle.
  7. Each function is created with `SET lock_timeout = '5s'` as a clause of `CREATE FUNCTION`, so the limit also covers the idempotency insert of item 1 (a `set local` in the body would come too late for it), and it catches `lock_not_available` (55P03) to raise `JV_BUSY`; the bound on the whole call is the role's `statement_timeout` (C4), because a `set local statement_timeout` inside a function does not shorten the call already running (tested); the screen retries `JV_BUSY` once with the same request id (S8). *Test:* T-C10 to T-C13, T-C15. *Source:* docs #7, #8, F1, RF-2, RM-6.
  8. **Test hook.** Each money function (X1 to X5, X10 to X12; the stock functions have no customer state) calls `private.test_pause('<function>.after_read')` once, after it has read the customer's state and before it allocates, and it allocates from what it read before the hook (a re-read after the hook would hide a missing lock); in production that function is empty. For a two-session test the hook is replaced by `create or replace` and **committed before the sessions start and restored after them** (an uncommitted replacement is invisible to the second connection); the replacement waits by polling `pg_try_advisory_lock` for less than 5 seconds, because the function's `lock_timeout` would cut a plain advisory wait; there is **one gate per session**, session 1 is held at the hook and session 2 is released first, and the test runs in both release orders. A plain wait on the customer row would not prove the lock, because the foreign key of the bill insert takes its own lock on the same row. *Test:* T-C09 to T-C14. *Source:* RM-8, RM3-2.
- **X1. `create_bill`** (owner, biller). Inputs: `p_request_id`, `p_customer_id`, `p_bill_date date` (null = today), `p_sale_type`, `p_items jsonb` (each: `sku_id`, `metal_rate`, `labour_rate`, `piroi`, `other_charges`, `quantity`; `metal_rate` is required; `labour_rate` is required for a By Weight line and ignored for a Per Piece line; an absent `piroi` or `other_charges` is 0 and an absent `quantity` is 1), `p_discount`, `p_additional`, `p_notes`, `p_payments jsonb` (each: `mode`, `amount`, `reference`), `p_confirm_held uuid[]`, `p_cash_override_reason text`. Steps after X0 items 1 to 3: (a) validate every input against S4, Y8, R6 and the payment modes (no `Credit`); (b) the customer must exist and not be archived (`JV_CUSTOMER_ARCHIVED`); (c) lock the pieces and refuse any that is not billable (R10) or already on an active bill (`JV_PIECE_UNAVAILABLE`, naming the pieces); (d) price the lines and the bill with R1 to R8 and R14 from the locked piece rows and the given rates, with the bill's pricing version (R13); (e) the explicit payments must not exceed `grand_total` (`JV_OVERPAYMENT`) and the cash rules of section 13 must pass; (f) take the bill number (L3, L4); (g) insert the bill and lines; (h) update the pieces to `Sold` with a conditional `UPDATE ... WHERE status is billable AND sold_bill_id IS NULL` and check the row count equals the number of lines (defence in depth); (i) insert one `money` receipt and one allocation per payment row, dated the bill date; (j) rebalance the customer (Y6), which applies any advance the customer holds; (k) store the result and return `{bill, items, receipts, allocations, warnings, advance_applied}`, where `warnings` may hold `PAN_MISSING`, `RATE_MISSING`, `RATE_STALE`. *Test:* T-A01 to T-A19, T-B03, T-B07, T-C04 to T-C06, T-C10, T-C11, T-D09. *Source:* hazards 1 and 2, INV-01, BIL-01 to BIL-06, BIL-11, F1, F14.
- **X2. `record_payment`** (owner, biller). Inputs: `p_request_id`, `p_customer_id`, `p_amount` (greater than 0), `p_mode` (Cash, UPI, Card, Bank Transfer, Other), `p_paid_on`, `p_reference`, `p_notes`, `p_bill_id` (optional), `p_cash_override_reason`. Inserts the receipt, allocates by Y3 (named bill first), keeps any remainder as an advance (for the walk-in customer a payment above its pending dues is refused, K5), applies the cash rules of section 13, rebalances (Y6), and returns `{receipt, allocations, advance_remaining}`. *Test:* T-B04 to T-B06, T-B23, T-C09, T-C10. *Source:* LED-01, LED-04, LED-05, LED-06, F9.
- **X3. `settle_fine`** (owner, biller). Inputs: `p_request_id`, `p_customer_id`, `p_grams`, `p_mode` (`Silver` or `Cash`), `p_silver_rate` (required for `Cash`), `p_bill_id` (optional), `p_paid_on`, `p_reference`, `p_notes`, `p_cash_override_reason`. Checks SF2, creates the `fine_silver` or `fine_cash` receipt (a `fine_cash` receipt carries `amount = round(grams × rate, 2)` and mode `Cash`), allocates grams by Y3, applies the cash rules to `fine_cash`, rebalances, and returns the receipt, allocations and the remaining fine. *Test:* T-B11 to T-B13, T-B23. *Source:* LED-02, LED-11, LED-12.
- **X4. `void_receipt`** (owner). Inputs: `p_request_id`, `p_receipt_id`, `p_reason` (at least 3 characters). The receipt must be `active` (`JV_ALREADY_VOID`). Marks it voided; its allocations stop counting; a voided refund restores the advance (Y10); then rebalances the customer (Y6), so money that was waiting as an advance now pays a bill that the void re-opened. It raises `JV_NOT_ALLOWED` ("void the refund first") when the customer's advance after the void would be below 0, because the money was already handed back; a payment that bounced after a refund is booked as dues with X12. Returns the receipt, the allocations made by the rebalance and the affected bills' derived status. *Test:* T-B08, T-B09, T-B20, T-C12, T-D09. *Source:* LED-02.
- **X5. `cancel_bill`** (owner). Inputs: `p_request_id`, `p_bill_id`, `p_reason` (at least 3 characters). The bill must be `active` (`JV_BILL_CANCELLED`); an `opening_balance` bill may be cancelled (IM5). For the walk-in customer a bill that has live allocations is refused with `JV_NOT_ALLOWED` (K5: void its receipts first). Applies K3, then rebalances (Y6). Returns the bill, the pieces returned to stock and the allocations released. *Test:* T-B10, T-C13, T-D09. *Source:* BIL-12.
- **X6. `create_customer`** (owner, biller). Inputs: `p_request_id`, name, mobile (required), customer type, email, address, GST number, PAN, notes. Creates the customer (never the walk-in). Returns the customer. Opening balances are X12. *Test:* T-D09, T-F06. *Source:* hazard 1 (a new customer is saved before the bill that names them), AC5.
- **X7. `quote_bill`** (owner, biller; read-only, no `p_request_id`, so it takes no locks and writes nothing). Inputs: `p_bill_date`, `p_sale_type`, `p_items jsonb`, `p_discount`, `p_additional`, `p_pricing_version` (default: the current one). Returns the lines, the totals, the list prices and the warnings that `create_bill` would produce, using the same `private.price_bill`. Used by tests and as a check against the screen's preview (C12). *Test:* T-A15.
- **X8. `create_design`** (owner, staff). Inputs: `p_request_id`, `p_design_id uuid` (made by the screen, P2; refused if it exists), `p_design jsonb` (category, subcategory, name, material, purity, selling method, description, notes, tags, photo paths), `p_pieces jsonb` (groups, each with `pieces` 1 to 100, weights, stones, kundan, piroi, three labour rates, notes; `original_net_weight` only if the caller is the owner, otherwise `JV_FORBIDDEN`). Creates the design (number from `design:<prefix>`), each piece (number from `sku:<design_no>`, barcode `<prefix>-<sku_no>`, status `In Stock`, location the first entry of `settings.locations`, which is the constant `BUSINESS_LOCATION` as coded, `AddDesignClient.tsx:1129`, AC3), and the owner-only weight rows; applies R1's blank-rate rule; skips numbers that already exist (L3). At most 500 pieces per call. Returns the design and pieces. *Test:* T-C03, T-C17, T-D03, T-F01. *Source:* INV-09, INV-10, hazard 4, F16.
- **X9. `set_sku_status`** (owner, staff). Inputs: `p_request_id`, `p_sku_ids uuid[]`, `p_status`, `p_note`. May set any status except `Sold`; a piece that is `Sold` cannot be changed (use X5 or X10). Setting `Archived` also sets `archived_at`; leaving `Archived` clears it (D8). Returns the updated pieces. *Test:* T-C07, T-C08, T-C17. *Source:* INV-01, `EditStockClient.tsx:152-154`, F7.
- **X10. `reissue_bill`** (owner). The **correction path**: cancel a wrong bill and save the right one in one transaction, keeping the money. Inputs: `p_request_id`, `p_old_bill_id`, `p_reason`, then the inputs of X1 except `p_customer_id` (the customer is the old bill's; it cannot change). The old bill must be `active` and `estimate` (a bill that is already cancelled, including one already re-issued, raises `JV_BILL_CANCELLED`, K6). Steps after X0: lock the pieces of the old bill and of the new lines together, by id ascending (X0 item 5); cancel the old bill (K3, without its own rebalance); create the new bill with `reissue_of = p_old_bill_id` and the rules of X1; then rebalance with **the new bill first**: the customer's unallocated money and silver grams, including what the cancelled bill had received, go to the new bill before any other bill (Y3 with a named bill), oldest receipt first; then the explicit new payments (X1(i)) are recorded, and the overpayment check counts what was carried over. For the walk-in customer a surplus of carried-over money over the new bill's total is refused (K5: void the receipts first). Returns both bills and the allocations. *Test:* T-B19, T-C13, T-D09. *Source:* F2, K1.
- **X11. `issue_refund`** (owner). **The first thing to leave M1 if the calendar slips.** Inputs: `p_request_id`, `p_customer_id`, `p_amount`, `p_mode`, `p_paid_on`, `p_reference`, `p_notes`. Under the customer lock, checks Y10 (amount not more than the customer's advance), inserts the `refund` receipt, rebalances (nothing to apply), and returns the receipt and the new advance. *Test:* T-B20, T-D09. *Source:* F2.
- **X12. `add_opening_balance`** (owner). Inputs: `p_request_id`, `p_customer_id`, `p_dues`, `p_fine_due`, `p_advance`, `p_fine_advance` (all at least 0, not all 0), `p_as_of date`. Used for corrections after the import (IM5) and for customers created later; the import itself runs the same `private.rebalance` (IM4). *Test:* T-B21, T-D09. *Source:* F4.

## 11. Derived views

All four views are `security invoker` (`WITH (security_invoker = true)`): the caller's row rules apply, because the default would use the view owner's (docs #9, lint 0010). The owner and the biller can read them; staff and `anon` cannot (A4). Nothing in them is stored (S3). Each is read in pages of 1,000 in a total order, so paging is stable (C3). V1 and V2 expose the base row's `updated_at`, which the touch triggers move whenever a payment or an allocation changes (D23, C4).

Two words used below. A **live allocation** is a `receipt_allocations` row whose `released_at` is null and whose receipt is `active`. Money allocations come from `money` receipts and carry `amount`; fine allocations come from `fine_silver` and `fine_cash` receipts and carry `fine_grams` (their `amount` is 0). A `refund` receipt never has allocations.

- **V1. `bill_summary`**: one row per bill: `id`, `bill_no`, `customer_id`, `bill_date`, `doc_type`, `sale_type`, `status`, `reissue_of`, `grand_total`, `received` (the sum of live money allocations to the bill), `pending`, `fine_due` (`fine_weight_due`), `fine_settled` (the sum of live fine allocations), `fine_pending`, `payment_status` (R11), `item_count`, `created_at`, `updated_at`. For an active bill `pending = grand_total - received` and `fine_pending = fine_due - fine_settled`; for a cancelled bill `received`, `pending`, `fine_settled` and `fine_pending` are all 0 (K3 released its allocations). *Test:* T-B02, T-B10, T-B15.
- **V2. `customer_balances`**: one row per customer, the walk-in customer included, keyed by `id` (the customer's id, which the cache merges by). Rupees: `billed` (sum of `grand_total` of active bills, opening bills included), `total_purchases` (the same without `opening_balance` bills), `received` (sum of `amount` of active `money` receipts, opening advances included), `refunded` (sum of active `refund` receipts), `allocated` (sum of live money allocations), then `outstanding = billed - allocated`, `advance = received - allocated - refunded`, and `balance = billed - received + refunded`. Always `balance = outstanding - advance`; after Y6 at most one of `outstanding` and `advance` is above zero (I10), and then `outstanding = max(0, balance)` and `advance = max(0, -balance)`. Silver: `fine_billed` (sum of `fine_weight_due` of active bills), `fine_received` (sum of `fine_grams` of active fine receipts), `fine_allocated`, `fine_outstanding = fine_billed - fine_allocated`, `fine_advance = fine_received - fine_allocated`, `fine_balance = fine_billed - fine_received`. Also `last_purchase` (the latest `bill_date` of an active, non-opening bill) and the customer row's `updated_at`. `outstanding` and `advance` come from allocations while `balance` comes from totals; the two routes must agree (I4, I10). *Test:* T-B05, T-B15, T-B17, T-B20, T-B21.
- **V3. `customer_ledger`**: one row per event of a customer, with a running `balance` and `fine_balance` (debit minus credit, summed in event order, `partition by customer_id`, so a filter on one customer is applied before the sums). Events: `Invoice` for each bill, cancelled ones too (`Opening` for an `opening_balance` bill): dated `bill_date`, debit `grand_total`, fine debit `fine_weight_due`. `Cancellation` for each cancelled bill: dated the day it was cancelled, credit `grand_total`, fine credit `fine_weight_due`. `Payment` for each `money` receipt (`Opening advance` when `is_opening`): dated `paid_on`, credit `amount`. `Fine` for each `fine_silver` or `fine_cash` receipt: dated `paid_on`, fine credit `fine_grams`, no rupee entry (for `fine_cash` the description says "settled in cash at rate X = amount Y"). `Refund` for each `refund` receipt: dated `paid_on`, debit `amount`. `Void` for each voided receipt: dated the day of the void, the exact reverse of its own row. **The order key** is (`entry_date`, `entry_at`, `kind_rank`, `id`): `entry_at` is the row's `created_at` (`cancelled_at` or `voided_at` for the last two kinds); `created_at` of bills and receipts is set with `clock_timestamp()`, so rows made in one transaction already differ; `kind_rank` makes the order total when two rows still tie (Invoice 1, Cancellation 2, Payment and Fine 3, Refund 4, Void 5), and `id` is the last tie-break. After the last row, `balance` equals V2's `balance` and `fine_balance` equals V2's `fine_balance` (I4). *Test:* T-B14, T-B25. *Source:* LED-04, LED-10, F18.
- **V4. `daily_totals`**: one row per (`business_date`, `mode`) for the modes Cash, UPI, Card, Bank Transfer and Other, with `money_in`, `money_out` and `net = money_in - money_out`. A `money` or `fine_cash` receipt adds `money_in` on its `paid_on` (so the Cash row includes silver settled in cash, Y1). A `refund` adds `money_out` on its `paid_on`. A voided receipt adds the **reverse** amount on the day of the void (a voided payment adds `money_out`, a voided refund adds `money_in`; an `is_opening` receipt, which V4 never counted, adds nothing), so a void never changes a day already closed (a back-dated receipt or refund by the owner, Y8, does: O3). Opening advances (`is_opening`) are not money received today and are left out (D15). The owner's evening check is three figures: today's Cash `net`, today's UPI `net`, and the sum of V2 `outstanding`. *Test:* T-B15, T-B16, T-B20, T-B21. *Source:* reviewer risk 1, F4.
- **V5. One set of selectors.** The dashboard, the reports and the customer card compute their figures from V1 to V4 through a single browser module, `src/lib/selectors.ts` (C14). **Sales** for a business date or month are the sum of `grand_total` of V1 rows with `status = 'active'` and `doc_type <> 'opening_balance'`, grouped by `bill_date`; cancelled bills are shown on their own line (count and value; a cancelled `opening_balance` bill is not counted there, because an opening-balance bill is never a sale, C14) and are not sales; a cancelled bill therefore leaves the day it was billed on (sales are a report, and only the cash book of V4 is kept stable for closed days). The module has a unit test over a fixed data set that checks every figure. *Test:* T-B15. *Source:* LED-10, DAT-08.

## 12. Invariants and the nightly check

`private.run_invariants()` returns the rows that break each rule as (`invariant`, `row_ref`, `detail`); an empty result is a pass. It only reads. It has one snapshot of the data for its whole run (the caller starts a `REPEATABLE READ` transaction; how `pg_cron` starts one is verified in the plan, open item (a) of section 0), so a payment saved while it runs cannot cause a false alarm.

- **I1.** Every `Sold` piece has a `sold_bill_id` of an `active` bill that has a line for it, and every line of an active bill points to a `Sold` piece with that bill id. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18.
- **I2.** No piece is on the lines of two active bills. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18.
- **I3.** For every active bill, live money allocations never exceed `grand_total` and live fine allocations never exceed `fine_weight_due`; for every active receipt, its live allocations never exceed its `amount` (money) or its `fine_grams` (fine); no allocation that is not released points to a cancelled bill; a fine allocation has `amount` 0 and a money allocation has `fine_grams` 0. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18.
- **I4.** For every customer, `balance` and `fine_balance` of V2 equal an independent recomputation from the base tables (`billed - received + refunded`, and the same for silver) and equal the last running balances in V3. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18.
- **I5.** Bill numbers are unique and, within each series (prefix and year, and `OB-`), the numbers issued by a function (those with no `import_batch_id`) are contiguous from the series' `seed_value + 1` (`seed_value` is 0 for a new series and is set by `private.seed_counters()` after an import, IM3), so an `OB-` number made by X12 after the import does not break the check; `seed_counters()` only raises a `seed_value`, and numbers at or below it are ignored. *Test:* T-B18, T-C16. *Source:* RF-6, RF3-5.
- **I6.** For every bill that has lines (opening bills have none, IM4): each stored line amount (`metal_amount`, `labour_amount`, `fine_weight_due`, `item_total`) equals a fresh run of `private.price_line` for the bill's `pricing_version` over the stored snapshot (R13); `subtotal` equals the sum of the lines and `fine_weight_due` the sum of the lines' fine due; `grand_total` equals `round(subtotal - discount + additional_charges, 0)`; and `round_off` is either 0 or `grand_total - (subtotal - discount + additional_charges)`, whichever the setting at the time of saving gave (so changing the setting later cannot fail the check). *Test:* T-B18, T-A15, T-C16.
- **I7.** No negative amount, weight or quantity exists in any table (except `round_off`, D11), no receipt has the mode `Credit`, no photo value starts with `data:`, and every `kind`, `mode` and `status` combination is one the checks of D13 allow (a guard in case a constraint is lost in a later migration). *Test:* T-B18.
- **I8. Nightly job.** At 02:00 Asia/Kolkata (20:30 UTC; open item (a) of section 0) `pg_cron` runs `run_invariants()` and writes a `check_runs` row (`ok`, and `details` with the count per invariant and at most 20 sample rows each), then deletes `private.idempotency` rows older than 90 days (L6). Nobody is paged in M1: the owner sees the dashboard line (below) whenever she opens it, and Harsh reads the check each morning for the first two weeks (plan task P4-T1). *Test:* T-B18. *Source:* reviewer risk 1.
- **I9.** The same function runs inside the test suite after random sequences of operations (T-B14) and after deliberate damage (T-B18), so a rule that drifts fails in tests before it can fail at the counter. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18.
- **I10. The rebalance post-condition (Y6).** For every customer: the sum of V1 `pending` over their active bills equals V2 `outstanding`, and the sum of V1 `fine_pending` equals `fine_outstanding`; `outstanding`, `advance`, `fine_outstanding` and `fine_advance` are not negative; if `advance` is above zero then `outstanding` is 0, and if `fine_advance` is above zero then `fine_outstanding` is 0; and the walk-in customer's `advance` and `fine_advance` are 0 (K5). *Test:* T-B14, T-B18, T-B26, T-C10, T-C11. *Source:* F1 (case B).
- **I11. The review line (a report, not an invariant; it never turns the check red).** For the previous business day: the bill lines sold below the server's list prices (R14), the bills with a discount above `private.limits.discount_flag_percent` (10) percent of the subtotal, the bills and receipts saved with a cash override reason (Z1), the cancellations, re-issues, voids and refunds, the walk-in's cash total and number of bills of the day (Z2 does not add them up, so this is where a split sale shows), the `fine_cash` settlements whose rate is more than `private.limits.rate_flag_percent` (5) percent away from every Silver rate of that day (the latest rate dated not after it, per purity; no Silver rate at all counts as away), the daily rates entered by a biller that moved more than that percent from the previous rate of the same material and purity, and the bills whose sale type differs from the customer's type (R9). The counts go into `check_runs.details`; the owner can open the lists (up to 50 rows each). Nothing is blocked (O26). *Test:* T-B24. *Source:* F12.
- **I12. Backup heartbeat (B2).** The dashboard line shows how many hours ago the last `backup_runs` row with `ok` for `db_dump`, and for `photo_copy`, was written. *Test:* T-R03. *Source:* F15.
- **I13. Cash book tie-out.** For each mode, the sum of V4 `net` over all dates equals the sum of the active `money` and `fine_cash` receipts of that mode that are not `is_opening`, minus the active refunds of that mode. It guards the void-day logic of V4. *Test:* T-B14, T-B18. *Source:* RM-13.

**The dashboard line (owner only)** shows, in one place: "Books: OK at 02:00" or "Problem" (I8), the age of the last good dump and photo copy (I12), and yesterday's review counts (I11). **Mutation tests (T-B18).** For each of I1 to I7, I10 and I13 a test, as the database owner inside a transaction that is rolled back, damages exactly one row (a constraint may be dropped inside that transaction) and expects `run_invariants()` to name that invariant and that row; with no damage it returns nothing. I13 reads the same rows as V4, so no single damaged row can fire it: its test replaces V4 with the old, wrong definition inside the transaction (a void of an opening receipt reversing money V4 never counted) and expects I13 to fire.

## 13. Cash limit and PAN prompt

Not legal or tax advice. The figures, the comparison (at or above, or above) and the legal posture are for Siddhi's CA `[CA]`; the reading used here comes from secondary sources only (research row 19, open item (h) of section 0). The thresholds are constants in `private.limits` (`cash_limit = 200000`, `pan_threshold = 200000`, `discount_flag_percent = 10`, `rate_flag_percent = 5`) so a decision changes one number in one migration.

- **Z1. Cash from a named customer.** For a customer that is not the walk-in, let `cash_today` be the sum of `amount` of their active receipts with `paid_on` on one business date that are `money` receipts of mode `Cash` or `fine_cash` receipts, leaving out `is_opening` rows and not reduced by refunds. When a function (X1 for the cash payment rows of a new bill, X10 through the rules of X1, X2 for a `Cash` payment, X3 for `fine_cash`) would make `cash_today` **reach `cash_limit` or more**, it raises `JV_CASH_LIMIT`, unless the caller is the **owner** and passes `p_cash_override_reason` of at least 10 characters. A biller cannot override: the owner enters that payment. The reason is saved on the bill (`cash_override_reason`) or on the receipt (`cash_override_reason`) and audited (L1, I11). Only the per-day total is built. Not built: the limb for one transaction paid in several parts, possibly over several days (which a per-day sum does not see), and the limb for one event or occasion (O14). *Test:* T-A12, T-B23. *Source:* CMP-08, F9.
- **Z2. The walk-in customer.** The walk-in row stands for many different people, so its cash is never added up over a day. Instead: a single cash payment (one `record_payment`, one `fine_cash` settlement, or the cash rows of one bill) of `cash_limit` or more for the walk-in raises `JV_CASH_LIMIT` with the message "Select or create the customer"; and X1 refuses a bill whose `grand_total` is `pan_threshold` or more for the walk-in with `JV_NOT_ALLOWED` and the same message (the PAN rule says "exceeding"; the stricter reading is deliberate and O14 asks the CA). There is no override for the walk-in: the owner creates the customer first. The walk-in never holds a PAN (D10). *Test:* T-A12, T-B23. *Source:* K5, F9.
- **Z3. PAN prompt.** When a bill's `grand_total` is `pan_threshold` or more and the named customer has no `pan`, X1 still saves the bill and returns the warning `PAN_MISSING`; the screen asks for the PAN and saves it on the customer (a content column, A5). A PAN is ten characters: five capital letters, four digits, one capital letter; anything else is refused by the `CHECK` on the customer row (D10), and the screen validates before it sends. It is a prompt, not a block, because a buyer may legitimately have no PAN and give a declaration instead. *Test:* T-A12. *Source:* CMP-08.
- **Z4.** Neither rule creates a new printable document (Harsh's ruling on "documents"); a buyer's declaration, if one is needed, is kept on paper outside the system `[CA]`. *Test:* none. *Source:* Harsh's ruling of 5 October.

## 17. Out of scope, and room left for M2

**Out of scope here:** see 1.1. **Room left, so M2 needs no breaking migration:**

- **Edit a bill (BIL-08, BIL-09, DAT-02, DAT-03).** An `amend_bill` function replaces the lines with the same pricing code (R12), keeps every receipt and allocation, re-derives balances (they are derived), and keeps the old version in a `bill_revisions` table. It replaces cancel and re-issue as the usual correction (plan task P5-T4). The `bills`, `bill_items` and allocation tables need no change. When the new total is below what is already allocated, the surplus allocation must be released and Y6 applies the money again: that rule is decided when M2 is specified.
- **Order Book.** An `orders` table numbered from `order:<YYYY>`, and advances that are ordinary `money` receipts linked to an order, so they reach the ledger (LED-14).
- **Public catalogue.** Anonymous read through row rules limited to in-stock, non-archived rows and column grants limited to safe columns, rather than a definer view (lint 0010). Never `sku_private`, never any cost or weight that is not already public. A private bucket is added then if needed (O11).
- **Returns and credit notes.** `doc_type = 'credit_note'` with a link to the original bill and the piece returned to stock; partial returns; a silver refund (O24).
- **Tax invoices.** `doc_type`, `gst_percent`, `gst_amount`, and the line `tax_rate` and `tax_amount` columns already exist; HSN and the document numbering series are added when decision 4 is made `[CA]`.
- **Staff screen.** A secured Edge Function that holds the service key for creating logins and resetting passwords; the key never reaches the browser. Until then Harsh uses the runbook snippet (U3).
- **Tags and scanning.** `skus.barcode` stays text, so a short numeric Code 128 value fits without a migration (plan tasks P5-T1, P5-T7).
- **Stock search and Excel upload.** Both call `create_design` (X8) or the same insert path; no schema change (plan tasks P5-T2, P5-T3).
- **A rule for one event or occasion** in the cash limit (O14), **financial-year numbering** (O31) and a **monthly sweep of photos that belong to no design** (P6).
- **Realtime** instead of polling, and a **bill-revision viewer** on the audit log (plan task P5-T8): nothing in the schema blocks them.
